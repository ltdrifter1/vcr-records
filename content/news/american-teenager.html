Preacher’s Daughter didn’t arrive as vibes. It arrived as a highway you can’t shuffle: motels, diners, a Harley cooling in a parking lot, church weather that doesn’t care about your outfit.

“American Teenager” sticks in the throat first. Then “Gibson Girl,” “Strangers,” “Sun Bleached Flies” — long on purpose. Ethel Cain came out, the church made it a problem, and the record treats that as climate, not a press angle.

In Cascadia you play this with the heater fighting the rain and pretend you’re not crying at a red light. You don’t put this on at a pre-drink unless you want the pre-drink to become a testimony.

Keep the aux. Take the long way. Let “Strangers” finish before anybody asks what’s next. Some records are doors. This one is a road that refuses the exit.
