“What I Want” is the floor that actually asks. MUNA on Phoebe’s Saddest Factory — three women making synth-pop that remembers the eighties without hiring a man to produce the memory. “Silk Chiffon” taught the timeline the pastel. This album is the night after: “Kind of Girl,” “Anything But Me,” the kind of chorus that shows up already knowing your ex’s name.

They left the major and they kept the hook. That’s the gossip that matters more than the press cycle. In this province it’s a kitchen with a cheap disco bulb, coats on the chair, somebody crying-laughing in the hallway because the lyric said the quiet part at festival volume.

You don’t analyse MUNA at 1:40. You turn it up until the neighbour knocks and then you text sorry like you meant it. What I want isn’t subtle. That’s why it works. The room finally stops negotiating.
