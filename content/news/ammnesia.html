On 3 April 1989, Jack Trax in the UK put out a double LP called Ammnesia, catalogue FING 2, and licensed it from Trax Records. Discogs credits Larry Heard with producing, writing and arranging it. The title is short a letter. The record went out with the misspelling on it, and for a long time that was the album people meant when they named his early singles as a single object.

The tracklist is the early work, gathered. "Can You Feel It," "Washing Machine," "Beyond the Clouds," "Slam Dance," "Stars," "Waterfalls," "Let's Dance All Night," "Bye Bye," "For So Long," the title track, "The Juice," "Mystery of Love." Ben Cardew, writing in DJ Mag on 5 October 2022, says two of those songs were new to the album, "Let's Dance All Night" and "Bye Bye," and that the rest had already been singles. He also says "Beyond the Clouds" was the third track on the 12-inch that carried "Washing Machine" and "Can You Feel It."

Heard was born on 31 May 1960, on the South Side of Chicago. Wikipedia, citing Sean Bidder's chapter in the Rough Guide to house, gives him a government job as a benefit authorizer, and says the pay is what bought the first studio gear. At 17 he was the drummer in Infinity, a jazz-fusion cover band that included Adonis. He left in 1984. Alexander Dias, writing for Roland on 17 February 2021, says Heard bought a Juno-60 and a TR-909 that year with savings from the government job, and that within a few days he had made three pieces that stayed. Wikipedia, citing John Bush's AllMusic biography, names them: "Can You Feel It," "Mystery of Love" and "Washing Machine."

Josh Baines interviewed him for Vice, over Skype, for a piece published on 27 June 2017. "Mystery of Love" had come out in 1985 on Heard's own Alleviated Music. The "Washing Machine" release was the one that put him on Trax, and Baines calls "Can You Feel It" the B-side of that single. Heard told him the song used a Roland Juno-60 and a TR-909 and nothing else. There were no digital recorders and no multitrack. He had two cassette decks. One take, one pass, onto one tape, then back onto the other while he played the next parts by hand. "It wasn't exactly the Beatles," he said.

Getting the cassette to the right person was the rest of the job. Heard told Baines the point was "getting it to Ron Hardy and Frankie Knuckles and Wayne Williams and people like that around town, people synonymous with party events." He had not started out knowing Knuckles. "I was just another bright-eyed kid coming up to him with a record." The Warehouse piece on this desk is the other side of that sentence, the resident. Heard's side is the kid with the tape, one of several who would have been in the line on a given afternoon.

The six notes

He met the singer and DJ Robert Owens at a party. With Owens and Ron Wilson he formed Fingers Inc., and in 1988 the group released the double LP Another Side. The Mr. Fingers singles kept coming alongside it, on Trax and on DJ International. Ammnesia is what happened when a UK label stacked the early solo records and two new songs and gave the pile a title with a letter missing.

Baines, listening back, describes "Can You Feel It" as a six-note bassline, a percussion track, and a few pads. Cardew counts the same six notes and says they never land where a listener expects them. The chords, in his account, carry a melancholy, as if the good time were already being remembered while it happens. On "Washing Machine" he hears the kick arrive slightly late, enough that the downbeat still surprises after years of knowing the record. "Stars," he writes, is a couple of synth figures and an effect worked hard. "Slam Dance" is drum-machine funk with the rest of the arrangement mostly declined. The overdubs, on Heard's account, were a second pass between two cassette decks. That was the studio.

Cardew's larger claim is that these records moved house out of the club and into ordinary listening: headphones, a living room, a repeat play with nobody dancing. He sets that against Jesse Saunders' "On and On," 1984, which he calls widely considered the first house track, so that "Washing Machine," two years later, is still early, and already asking a different thing of the music than a jack track had to answer. The part that survives an argument about origins is the part both writers are actually describing. Six notes, set off the grid. Chords with a melancholy under them. A kick that comes in late.

The letter, then the signature

The 1989 LP was not his edition. Wikipedia, citing Bush, says Trax issued Ammnesia without Heard's permission. Mixmag Asia states the same. The object Discogs describes is the Jack Trax pressing, licensed from Trax, under the misspelled name. In 2022 Heard's own label, Alleviated, put the album out as Amnesia, the vowel restored. Word and Sound's page for the CD, catalogue ML9002CD, dates it 27 June 2022 and calls that issue the first time the album was officially available. Cardew, that October, notes that the reissue cleared the spelling. From the Jack Trax date to the Alleviated date is thirty-three years. The bassline had not been waiting on the paperwork.

One measure of how far the six notes travelled: Cardew writes that Kanye West sampled "Mystery of Love" on "Fade," in 2016. The sample is a later fact about a 1985 record. It does not improve the cassette. It does show that a line played once, on a synth Heard has named, was still identifiable enough to lift.

Gerd Janson asked him, in a 2005 Red Bull Music Academy interview, whether he had known at the time that he was onto something. Cardew quotes the answer. "Oh no," Heard said. There is no way to know in advance. You aim at something people like, with some longevity to it, and you cannot plan that, or every record would be a classic. The people listening decide. He could not decide it for them.

Jack Trax printed Ammnesia. Alleviated was willing to sign Amnesia. A listener meets the same take under either spelling: one pass, then the tape run back, then a hand adding the next part, on a Juno-60 and a 909. The authorization is a question of custody. It does not rewrite the pass. The pass had already left the house, under a title with a letter missing, and the letter was never what people were dancing to, or sitting with, when they asked for the record by the wrong name and got the right one.
