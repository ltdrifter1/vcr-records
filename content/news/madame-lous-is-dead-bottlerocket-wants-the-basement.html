Madame Lou's is dead. Here-After is dead. Say it without the soft focus.

Mid-December 2025, the Crocodile closed the two smaller rooms (Madame Lou's at something like three hundred capacity, Here-After at something like one hundred) and cut about a hundred jobs. The Seattle Times had the motto, and the motto did the work the press release would not: "We built a venue of the future, and the future didn't land in 2025."

The future not landing is a very Seattle sentence. It sounds atmospheric. About a hundred people lost their jobs.

If you danced downstairs last year you already know which future they meant. Not the tourist one. The one where a mid-size room still booked the weird bill and the booth still belonged to someone who cared about a Tuesday. Madame Lou's was the room you told out-of-town DJs they could have. Here-After was the smaller swear: a hundred bodies, a second door, the after that still had a legal address. Both gone in the same mid-December. One building, two holes, a hundred people looking at a calendar that no longer had their names on it.

The Stranger, 18 December 2025, put the wider number on the table: NIVA's figure that roughly forty percent of Washington indie venues were profitable. Forty percent. Sit with that if you have ever watched a door-person count heads like it was a moral test. Most of the rooms are not making it. The ones that look like they are making it are often one bad quarter from a motto about the future.

Then the sale. Comedy Tent (Jimmy Miller and Mike McAvoy, the UCB / Abso Lutely / Bottlerocket names) bought the operation. Secondary coverage put something like $1.6 million in debt on the desk. Night Shift will do what the coverage did and leave the exact spreadsheet alone. Debt figures in sale pieces are not a venue's own books handed to a zine. They are a number that travelled. File it as coverage, not as gospel. The ownership is the part that does not need a hedge. Comedy bought the building's next life.

Comedy Tent buying the basement is a transfer of who owns the floor.

31 August 2026, the answer got a name. The basement becomes Bottlerocket Social Hall: something like four hundred capacity, target March 2027, dance, comedy, bands. Pittsburgh programming (KIRO and the Puget Sound Business Journal carried the plan). A social hall is a sentence that wants to be for everybody. Everybody is how a room stops being for you.

March 2027 is a long winter from here. You will hear the words dance floor in the listings before you hear a kick that sounds like this city. You will hear comedy in the same breath, which is honest. Bottlerocket is a comedy operation walking into a basement that used to hold the overflow night. They are not hiding that. The mix, as announced, is dance plus comedy plus bands, a Pittsburgh program mind in a downtown hole. Whether that mix can hold a house night, a jungle night, an instrumental hip-hop bill that does not need a punchline is not in the announcement. Announcements do not owe you a booth philosophy.

Who owns the floor. That is the only question this city should be asking, and it almost never does.

Neither smaller room survived 2025, and the jobs did not survive it either. A hundred cuts is a scene losing the people who knew how to run a door at 11:40 when the bill was late.

Portland, Vancouver, and Seattle do not have spare mid-size rooms. Portland just spent a decade without a dedicated all-ages hall. Vancouver is inspecting the ones it has. Seattle just watched two of them become a sale. If you live anywhere on this wet strip and you care about a dance floor that is not a brand activation, you are counting remaining addresses on one hand and you are not being dramatic.

NIVA's forty percent is the cold math under the gossip. Most indie rooms in this state are not profitable. The Crocodile built smaller rooms anyway, then closed them, then sold the future to a comedy shop that wants a social hall in the basement by next March. That sequence is what happens when the future is a business plan and the floor is a line item.

What is on the page is enough: two rooms closed, a hundred jobs, a motto that admitted the landing missed, a sale to Comedy Tent, a debt figure in the coverage, a basement with a new name, a four-hundred cap, a March 2027 target, and a mix that puts dance in a list with bits and bands.

Madame Lou's and Here-After closed in mid-December 2025. About a hundred jobs went with them. Comedy Tent bought the operation. The basement's new name is Bottlerocket Social Hall, about four hundred capacity, target March 2027. If you need a dance floor in Seattle this winter, it is not downstairs at Madame Lou's.
