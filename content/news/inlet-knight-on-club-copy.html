A two-track single in August, then a run of one-track records in October, with Pitch Black the release grouped as an album.

The tags on the August single are the ones worth sitting with. Deep house, lo-fi house, and UK garage get filed as if they were separate rooms. Putting them on one short record describes how a lot of house gets made now: a dusty source, a UK swing, a patience about when the bass arrives. Whether these tracks earn every tag is testable. They are on the page. The October titles are plainer than the tags. please wait. try to love again. run it back. Any of them could be said across a kitchen.

Cumberland is a town on Vancouver Island. UK garage, as a tag, names a swing and a bass weight people learned from British records in the 1990s and have argued about since. Using it in 2026 on a Pacific Northwest account says which records this page wants to be heard beside. The bio does not expand on the choice. It states a town and a music. Played in release order, the page moves from a named single in August to a week in October with a new title on almost every day. That week has what a signing line never did: titles, dates, and a town that matches on the bio and the location line.
