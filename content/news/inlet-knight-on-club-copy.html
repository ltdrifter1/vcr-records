Forsberg’s Bandcamp bio is one sentence. “House music from Cumberland, BC.” The location line names the same town: Cumberland, British Columbia. On j’adore, the earliest release still on the music page, the tags are deep house, lo-fi house, and uk house. That is the self-portrait. The discography is now long enough to read beside it.

j’adore came out on 10 August 2026. Two tracks: don’t cry, and j’adore. Pitch Black came out on 18 October 2026, three tracks: Neighbourhood Spy, Primo, Pitch Black. The same week the page filled with singles. Please Wait on 17 October. Eighteen titles. call anytime and Freed From (Nanana) are not on the music page. A two-track single in August, then a run of one-track records in October, with Pitch Black the release grouped as an album.

The tags on the August single are the ones worth sitting with. Deep house, lo-fi house, and UK house get filed as if they were separate rooms. Putting all three on one short record describes how a lot of house gets made now: a dusty source, a UK swing, a patience about when the bass arrives. Whether these tracks earn every tag is testable. They are on the page. The October titles are plainer than the tags. Safe As. Deeper. Please Wait. Try To Love Again. Outta This Mess. Any of them could be said across a kitchen.

Cumberland is a town on Vancouver Island. UK house, as a tag, names a swing and a bass weight people learned from British records in the 1990s and have argued about since. Using it in 2026 on a Pacific Northwest account says which records this page wants to be heard beside. The bio does not expand on the choice. It states a town and a music. Played in release order, the page moves from a named single in August to a week in October with a new title on almost every day. That week has what a signing line never did: titles, dates, and a town that matches on the bio and the location line.
