A mix is a different object from a catalogue number. Nobody is asked to pay, the running order is the form, and if the track list is not printed the listener is either trusted or left without a citation. NIGHTSHIFT Vol. 3 leaves the second reading open. It is on the Club Copy mixes page, from the LT Drifta SoundCloud, titled “NIGHTSHIFT (VOL.3),” with the public description “a few days off....” The runtime in the mixes data is 1:19:57. The SoundCloud time is 2 October 2026, 05:29 UTC, which is 1 October 2026 at 10:29 p.m. Pacific. The permalink is soundcloud.com/ltdrifta/nightshift-vol-3. There is no public track list on the mixes page. This piece will not invent one, and it will not describe a transition it cannot name.

The series around it is short and recent, which is the useful part. Vol. 0, Vol. 1, and Vol. 2 carry SoundCloud times on 29 September 2026, between 04:51 and 05:22 UTC — the evening of 28 September on a Pacific clock. Vol. 0 runs 1:59:26 and is described “~ g h o s t l y ~.” Vol. 1 runs 1:01:17 and is described “~ finishing move ~.” Vol. 2 sits in the same half-hour. The runtime stored for it, 16:15:37, is not a figure this piece will repeat. A sixteen-hour mix posted inside that window is a number that needs a correction before it needs a review. Vol. 3 arrives three days later by the UTC stamp, shorter than Vol. 0, longer than Vol. 1, with a plainer sentence for a description. Four volumes, two Pacific evenings, and a title shared with this magazine. Which name came first is not a fact on either page. Both exist. A review can say that without a myth about who borrowed from whom.

The tags are the only contents note the public object has. The mixes data, taken from the SoundCloud track page, lists House, Deep House, Drum & Bass, jungle, UK garage, and Hip-hop & Rap. That stack is the sentence the release pages keep splitting apart. Champion Sound is filed as instrumental hip-hop. Classic Jazz is filed as jazz. Summer Madness is filed as electronic. The artist page says jungle. Vol. 3’s tags put house, jungle, garage, drum and bass, and hip-hop on a single upload of one hour and twenty minutes. Tags are not a transcript. An account can tag a mix for the neighborhood it wants, and the neighborhood can be wider than the hour. They are the only contents note on offer. A listener who does not DJ can use them as a map of intentions: this hour is being offered to more than one floor. Hip-hop and house do not share a tempo. Jungle and deep house rarely share a room. Putting the words on one mix is either a journey with a real sequence or a net cast so the upload can be found. Without a track list, those two readings cannot be separated. What can be said is that the account wanted all six words attached.

That want is part of the label’s story, and it is the opposite of the priced shelf. The records specialize. The mixes refuse to. On the mixes page the same account’s titles run in series: Deep in Tha Jungle, with years written into the names (1993–96, 1994 hardcore, 1996 atmosphere), Liquid Love, Loft Music, Café Après, a shoegaze and dreampop run called blissed out, Club Copy Radio, and NIGHTSHIFT. Forty uploads are listed. The five Club Copy titles are the part with a price and a catalogue number. The mixes are the part that shows the listening, and they dwarf the shelf. Treating Vol. 3 as a footnote to a four-minute single would invert the proportions. Four minutes and fifty-three seconds is a credo. An hour and twenty is a piece of a night. The description “a few days off....” is the only mood the upload itself claims, four dots included. Days off from what is not said. Next to a catalogue that keeps describing rooms, a mix can be the hours when the records are not being numbered.

How to hear it is ordinary, which is the point of writing it down. The mixes page carries the upload, and so does the SoundCloud permalink. An hour and twenty minutes is a sitting or a walk, not a single slipped between other singles. Heard next to the priced records, the contrast is scale. Heard next to Vol. 0 and Vol. 1, it is a series: the long one, the hour, the volume whose stored length this page will not certify, and then Vol. 3. A track list is not in the margin. If one is published later, a review can be revised. Until then the critical take stays with the frame.

The frame is enough to keep. A label whose Bandcamp address is Cumberland has a producer whose public work divides into priced records and a much larger run of mixes. One of those mixes uses the magazine’s name, lasts 1:19:57, and files itself under six genres the release pages do not allow to touch. That is a document. It is not a crate dig, because the crates are not shown. The limitation matters. The archive value this magazine says it wants comes from what a reader can recheck. A reader in five years can recheck the title, the time stamp, the runtime, the tags, and the fact that no track list was printed beside this piece in October 2026. They cannot recheck a transition the piece refused to fake. The records with titles worth arguing about are on the artist page, in catalogue order, for when the hour ends.
