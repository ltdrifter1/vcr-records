They sold a Tuesday arena show as a party, and the door is at six.

On 16 October the UK Pokémon site put the invitation in the headline. “Join the Pokémon Night Out Party.” Under it: “Are you ready to dance the night away?” The night has two buildings. The Intuit Dome in Los Angeles on 24 October, and The O2 in London on 10 November, “uniting the vast world of Pokémon with high-intensity dance music” through Marshmello, who headlines, and Alison Wonderland, who plays before him. London tickets were on sale. Tuesday 10 November 2026, doors at 6pm, through AXS, the ticket seller on the London date. Buy from the arena or from AXS and the ticket lives on a phone, inside The O2 app. The booking sits inside “What’s Your Favorite?”, the campaign for the brand’s 30th year.

On 13 April, in Bellevue, The Pokémon Company International called Pokémon Night Out a concert experience, two nights only, for fans aged 16 and up, and the first time the brand would “step into the global EDM spotlight,” electronic dance music, with custom sets and visuals made for the night. London went on sale at 10am BST on 17 April, through AXS. Los Angeles went on sale the same morning, Pacific time, through Ticketmaster. Taito Okiura, vice president of marketing and media, said the campaign was built to bring people together through a shared love of Pokémon, and that both artists “share a genuine passion for Pokémon that extends beyond the stage.”

On 2 October the US site told anyone staying home how to watch Los Angeles. The livestream is 24 October at 7:30pm Pacific, on the official Twitch and YouTube channels, from the Intuit Dome. London has no stream. The way into 10 November is a ticket.

There is also a bag. T-shirts, sweatshirts, jerseys, and a clear vinyl bag “for all your future concert excursions,” at Pokémon Center and in the on-site shop, with further pieces sold only in the building. Read the sentences in the order they arrived and the night changes its name. April called it a concert experience. The bag calls it a concert. Friday’s headline calls it a party. The O2 calls it a show with a 6pm door, a headliner and a support. What is on sale at Peninsula Square is an arena night with two names and a souvenir that assumes you will want another one.

The party has not earned its name. A passion for the cartoon does not turn an arena into a club, and a 6pm door on a Tuesday is a concert for whoever the 16+ line has cleared to buy one. Okiura’s sentence about a passion beyond the stage is polite. The sales pitch was fixed in April.

Someone in Victoria can watch Los Angeles for nothing, on a laptop, the night the Dome opens. London is a flight plus a phone ticket, for a show that starts at six. If you wanted a party, six o’clock is the part you did not mean to buy. The arena has already filed the night under show.
