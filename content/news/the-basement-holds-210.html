Sunset Labs published its interview with David Tillson on 5 June 2012. He managed Hush, the nightclub at 1325 Government Street in Victoria, at the south end of Vancouver Island. This version of the club, he said, was born on 1 January 2000, "the bastard child of the Limit and Rumors." The address had been an underground music bar since the late 1970s. Jazz and blues first, then a gay bar, then electronic music, until the room was sold in September 1998. Asked to describe it to someone who had never been, he called it "an old, beaten on, picked on, sometimes bullied, hole in the basement that concerns itself with the best in underground dance music." It held 210 people. "So there is always a tight budget," he said. Booking was a balancing act. They tried to concentrate on quality, and they booked local, national and international, "but still have to be conscious of rent day." In the same interview he said he was in his eighth year as president of the Pride festival. When the questions turned to famous guests, he asked if the interviewer wanted a name-drop, and then he did not give one. He had seen top-ranking DJs there, he said, and local talent that could hold their own. He had danced to great shows in a full room and to great shows in an empty one.

The calendar supplies the names he skipped. Seventeen days after the interview, the livevictoria archive of Hush's past events lists 22 June 2012 as "MARK FARINA!!!," with Lori J Ward, and with the listing pointing at markfarina.net and mushroomjazz.net. Wikipedia's summary of Farina calls him an American DJ known for Chicago house, acid jazz and downtempo, and for Mushroom Jazz, a series it dates from 1996 to 2016. It identifies him with the house scenes of Chicago, his hometown, and of San Francisco. The door in Victoria held 210. The June bill was a name that had already moved between those two cities.

On 3 November 2012 the same archive lists "DETROIT TECHNO - TECH HOUSE" at Hush, 10 p.m. to 1 a.m.: Orlando Voorn with Rennie Foster. Beside Voorn's name the listing prints a parenthesis and stops: Night Vision, KMS, Tresor, UR, NRK, Metroplex, and an etc. That parenthesis is the whole biography on the bill. The entry for 29 April 2013 is "WE PLAY VINYL *EVERY MONDAY*," David T, Big Tiny Smalls, Matt Rose and friends. The line under the date claims the habit in six words: "All vinyl, all night, every monday."

The last spring, and the room after it

The outside names continue past that winter. On 7 December 2013 the archive bills DJ Garth, "Los Angeles Wicked | Grayhound," with Mr Moe and Solomon Potashnick, at $12. The listing's note says that before moving to Los Angeles in 2010, Garth had spent two decades in San Francisco. On 4 April 2014 it bills Alix Perez with Tedder and Sinesthetix, at $15, and describes Perez as "Belgium born, London living." On 16 May 2014 it bills DJ Dubconscious, calling him a Shambhala ambassador, for "his final set at Hush before they," and the sentence on the page breaks off there. Victoria Music Scene, on 25 July 2024, dated Hush's final event to 26 July 2014. CHEK News, Adam Chan, the same day, reported that the club had shut in 2014. A May listing that says "final set" and a July date for the club's last event are not the same night. Both documents put the close in 2014.

Another Victoria room was still printing a Berlin credit three years later. The livevictoria page for 21 October 2017 lists Frequency Saturdays at the Copper Owl, 1900 Douglas Street, 10 p.m. to 2 a.m., presented by Indeed Entertainment. The bill is Adam Marshall, with "New Kanada, Berlin" in the line, and Rennie Foster again. The venue block gives the capacity as 120. On that same page the Copper Owl is marked Closed / Inactive. Indeed Entertainment, the presenter, is marked Open / Operational. A 120-cap room on Douglas Street could put a Berlin label on a Saturday listing. The page that preserves the listing also preserves the room's end.

Government Street came back as an announcement. On 25 July 2024 Chan reported that the owners of a new club, The Downbeat, said 1325 Government Street was slated to open in September. Just Beats Inc. was the company. DJ Daniel James helmed it, with Mark Williams and Karim Sayani. They hoped, Chan wrote, to fill a void in Victoria's live music after other spaces had closed, and he named Logan's Pub, shut in 2020. CHEK quoted the electronic webcast Victronic: for a decade "only the echoes of magic memories" had been left in the "formerly world famous" Hush, "before that it was named Rumours," a place "where a community was built upon underground sounds." Tillson's spelling, twelve years earlier, was Rumors. Victoria Music Scene, publishing the same day, spelled the third partner Karim Saryani, said Victronic had broken the news, and reported that James had formed Just Beats in Calgary and moved to Victoria in 2020. Describing his post, the Music Scene wrote that he saw the lease as a way to secure space for underground electronic music and for live music.

The date moved. Victoria Music Scene reported on 13 November 2024 that The Downbeat Event Centre would open on Friday 22 November, and that the venue had sat vacant for a decade. "New Beginnings," the debut, was listed for 7 p.m. that Friday: Daniel James, Natty, J-Feud, Ob1-Keoni, Villain Dafoe, Mark Williams, My Love and Billium. The Saturday bill belonged to Change The Beat, described there as a non-profit dedicated to empowering electronic music artists: Sydney Blu, Marlee, Tigress and Kookoomama, "an all woman lineup." The published opening weekend is those two pieces of paper.

What the postage was for

Vancouver is across the Strait of Georgia. The mainland record that lines up with these bills is a shop. Scout Magazine, on 11 December 2013, previewed a collectors' and selectors' convention for Sunday 15 December, 2 p.m. to 8 p.m., at Bestie in Chinatown. Pacific Rhythm, in Scout's account, was a trio of Dane Brown, a co-owner of Bestie, Derek Duncan and Russell Cunningham, working directly with small labels and distributors "from around the world." Brown's line, quoted there, put the afternoon on "in collaboration with our friends at Mood Hut, 1080p, and More Than Human."

Andrew Ryce, in Resident Advisor on 2 February 2015, wrote that the seller would open a shop on 8 February at 441 Gore Avenue, between East Hastings and East Cordova. The online store had started in 2013 so collectors in North America could buy records without European shipping rates. It had become a label and a party as well. Lnrdcroy's "Sunrise Market (Extended Mix)," from Rhythms of the Pacific Vol. 1, sat at number 39 in RA's top tracks of 2014. Ryce called Duncan, who DJs as D.Dee, the founder. Duncan's line in the piece was that they would be "Vancouver's premiere source for DJ records," boutique labels, with an emphasis on records exclusive to the shop. The opening party ran from 2 p.m. to 8 p.m.: Jack J and Liam Butler, the Pender Street Steppers, Hashman Deejay and Neo Image. On 21 February the next session was at the Fox Cabaret with Terekke, identified on the RA listing as L.I.E.S., New York.

The Georgia Straight's vinyl roundup of 3 December 2015 put 441 Gore among the city's shops and called Pacific Rhythm one of the first to specialize in 12-inch dance singles. Mail order had become a store "on the edge of Chinatown earlier this year." When it opened, the Straight wrote, it was the first of its kind in Vancouver in nearly a decade. The owner-operators in that paragraph are Duncan and Dane Brown. The Straight's name for what the counter had turned into is "the de facto clubhouse for the local dance-music scene."

City limits

Mood Hut's account of itself comes later, and it stays with the collective. Andy Beta's profile in Bandcamp Daily, 15 June 2022, is an interview with Jack Jutson, who releases as Jack J and as half of the Pender Street Steppers. Jutson says he is not there to speak for the group. Beta notes that the label had never agreed to interviews, and that there was not a Mood Hut remix. Jutson was born in Sydney. The family moved to Vancouver when he was 18. He started a short-lived band, No Gold, with Ian Wyatt and Liam Butler, both later members of Mood Hut. "Vancouver was and still is more of a live music kind of scene," he told Beta. "If you were DJing, you were either opening or playing between bands." The underground he describes was outside: a forest, a beach, a clifftop clearing, parties in the small hours, drawn from the David Mancuso Loft approach, which for him meant nice sound, balloons, a festive air. The band idea ended. The parties, the jam space and the cassettes they released themselves took the name Mood Hut.

C.Z. Wang, who also releases as People Plus, told Beta that Mood Hut was "foremost a collective," and that for a long time they did free parties in Stanley Park, "some of our most legendary parties." Jutson said those nights came close "to the potential of what dances can be," once the usual constraints were gone and people were willing to give themselves over. The cassette-length mixes, as Beta describes them, took cues from zoned-out Detroit house, New Age, dusty techno, Jamaican dub, smooth jazz and 1980s sophisti-pop, without settling on one of those. Yu Su, in the same piece, said she learned from the parties and the productions what "dance music that sounds grounded" could be: complex texture and rhythm "with a solid groove that connects to human emotions."

The ambition, in Jutson's telling, was the city. "We pretty much achieved everything and more that we had imagined we could do within the first year of putting out records. We weren't trying to go much further than our own city limits and sharing our music with our friends." Beta writes that most of the 12-inches carried the tag "It's That Sound!" The profile is hung on Opening the Door, which he calls the label's first single-artist full-length. Jutson had finished it in 2019. Pandemic delays and the pressing plants held it, and "this year," in a piece dated June 2022, is the release. He describes the time after "Thirstin'" as years of travelling, songs almost finished and then left because a tour intervened, until the stack no longer made sense as dance 12-inches. He wanted home listening, a brightness with a darkness in it. Yu Su's remark on the album is about melody, "the kind of emotions that slip away easily for the rest of us."

Lnrdcroy's "Sunrise Market" was already sitting on Resident Advisor's 2014 year-end list. Terekke's New York label was already on a Fox Cabaret bill. The year-end list and the Fox date are the distance the records travelled while the collective was still describing itself as local. In August 2025 the same name was still hiring guests. Rachel Leone, in Resident Advisor on 1 August 2025, reported the second season of Sound of Summer, the label and collective with Paradise Island, weekly on Granville Island until September, 5 to 11 p.m., at a new open-air pop-up called Chain & Forge Plaza. The 27 July opener was Jeremy Castillo with Josh Cheon. The listings under the piece date Kornél Kovács, with DJ Express and DJ HERA, to 16 August, and Yu Su, with Slim Media Player, to 29 August. The close, 12 September, is Space Ghost, Martyn Bootyspoon and DJ D.Dee. Leone's founding line says Duncan and Early, Russell Cunningham, started Pacific Rhythm in 2013, and calls it a label, a distributor and a party collective. The imprint's names in that sentence include D. Tiffany, Jack J, Lnrdcroy and Neo Image. Brown, an owner-operator in the Straight's 2015 paragraph and one of Scout's trio in 2013, is absent from the 2025 sentence. The door on Government Street held 210. The last Pacific Rhythm bill of that summer still had the founder's DJ name on it.
