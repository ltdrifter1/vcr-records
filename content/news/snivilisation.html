Snivilisation is Orbital’s third studio album, Paul and Phil Hartnoll, released by FFRR in Britain in August 1994. They recorded it across 1993 and 1994. The brown-sleeved Orbital album of 1993 had already made them an albums group rather than a rave act who happened to fill a CD. This one is darker and funnier. The title sneers at civilisation and then the music declines to be only a sneer: long tracks, acid lines, breaks, spoken fragments, pieces that change their mind halfway through and remain the same piece.

Are We Here? is the track with a future in it. Alison Goldfrapp sings on it, her first released vocal, years before the band that took her surname. Sad But True and Kein Trink Wasser are the other pieces that show the range, from a joke with a straight face to a club track that does not want a joke at all. Orbital’s public method by 1994 was already visible at gigs: two brothers, sequencers, a live show built from the same machines, tracks that could last as long as the room allowed. The album is that method with a title that picks a fight.

British electronic albums that summer were arguing with the Criminal Justice Act, some of them by name. Orbital’s argument is in the sneer and in the generosity. They were established enough to make a third album that did not repeat the brown sleeve, and generous enough to hand a vocal to a singer nobody had heard on a record yet. August 1994, FFRR, two brothers. Are We Here? is the question. She answers it.
