InterviewOct 27, 2022

CJ Archer

Where it started, the Pacific Northwest, and why the best records don’t ask where they’re supposed to fit.

There’s a certain kind of music that takes a while to find its way out. CJ Archer has spent years around it — records, half-finished projects, old machines, the Pacific Northwest version of an underground that never needed a coastal magazine to exist. We sat down (kettle, no publicist) to talk about Club Copy, the weather, and why a finished record is usually just a feeling somebody refused to throw away.

You started this from Vancouver. What does that actually sound like, besides rain?

It sounds like rooms that aren’t trying to be Brooklyn. Wet streets. A shop you can stand in too long. Ferries. The kind of night where the show is thirty people and somebody brought a cake. I like music that can live in that. If it only works in a caption, I’m not interested.

Club Copy is tiny on purpose. Is that a romance or a strategy?

Both, if I’m honest. Small editions mean you still know who might hold the thing. Catalogue numbers. A cassette that has to be dubbed. I’m not allergic to a bigger room — I just don’t want the music to start performing for one. The record club is the invite: first listens, a discount if you’re in, nothing shipped unless you choose it. That’s enough infrastructure for me.

Molly Haze, Rosco, L.T. Drifta, Riscape — how do you know when someone’s ours?

I know when I want to leave it on. Not when I can explain it. Molly’s from Cumberland — house that feels like a private temperature. Rosco’s Vancouver locked-drums, late-car energy. Drifta digs like it’s a practice. Riscape came in from Athens with piano in his hands and peak-time in his chest. None of them asked to match. They just all sound like they were finished by a person, not a moodboard.

What’s the part people get wrong about “underground”?

That it’s a costume. Or a purity test. Underground is just the work you do when nobody’s coming. Then if people come, you try not to sand off the thing that made you stay. Gossip, rooms, lyrics on trial, a club selling tickets and still saying no at the door — that’s all the same story. Who gets to keep the feeling, and who tries to checkout-button it.

Last one. What should somebody play tonight, if they’ve never been here?

j'adore if they want to be in a car. Gorilla if they want Molly looking them in the eye. The Drifta mixtape if they want the house to keep going after they think they’re done. And if they want the long memory: Donuts, then whatever we put out that still has dust on it.
