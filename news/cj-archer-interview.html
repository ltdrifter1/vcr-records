CJ Archer: A Short Conversation

There’s a certain kind of music that takes a while to find its way out.

CJ Archer has spent years around that kind of music. Records, mixes, half-finished projects, local scenes, old machines, new ideas. The Pacific Northwest has always had its own version of the underground, even when nobody outside the region was paying much attention.

For CJ, the attraction was never really about being current. It was about digging deeper. House, jungle, funk, soul, breaks. Records that connect to other records. Sounds that carry a little history without getting trapped in it.

That same approach runs through the work around Club Copy. The label is small by design, but the music comes from a much bigger place. L.T. Drifta, Inlet Knight, Double-Edge and others are part of a loose network of producers, DJs and friends making things because they want to hear them exist.

We caught up with CJ to talk about where it started, the Pacific Northwest, making records, running a label, and why the best stuff is usually made without worrying too much about where it’s supposed to fit.
