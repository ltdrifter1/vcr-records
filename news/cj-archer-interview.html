CJ Archer Interview

A short talk with CJ Archer.

Where did the name come from?

A kid in my family made it up. It stuck. CJ is plain. Archer is pointed. Together they confuse people a little, and I like that.

When did electronic music get serious for you?

Late ’90s. House twelves, jungle white labels, plus a lot of funk and soul. I didn’t see the whole culture until the early 2000s: DJing, raves, bedroom studios. Flash and Bambaataa still sat next to those piles. By the mid-2000s I was buying dance music and breaks almost exclusively. Later I bought records just to sample and finally understood where the beats came from.

Influences?

Look inside the crates.

How does it feel watching the Pacific Northwest underground get louder?

The coast has always had ups and downs. Early on, not much from up here traveled. Records sat in rooms and on burned CDs longer than they sat in shops. The blog and SoundCloud years brought great files out of Vancouver and the Island. Some got heard. A lot didn’t.

I used to make mixes with house on one side and jungle on the other. Just to keep the night moving. Then the bigger platforms showed up and putting out your own record started to feel like shouting into a feed.

In the 2010s, “regional” electronic music carried a stigma. That made the challenge more fun. We got more love from Europe and later Japan than from our own country. The last few years feel different. People are finally hearing it.

Who are you feeling right now?

L.T. Drifta on the beats. Inlet Knight and Double-Edge too. Different tempos, same honesty.

Enough support, or do more heads need to wake up?

Both. I’m grateful for the people who get the label. I also wish the records reached further. Ten people who really care beat a hundred who are kind of into it. I don’t want these in the dollar bin in a few years.

Producing or running the label?

Happy doing both. Hate the setup for both. Digging for a session can take ten hours. Then it’s wires, temperamental gear, and waiting for things to behave.

How do you define your style?

I don’t. Some songs sound like years ago. Others sound like 2030. Dated futuristic.

What’s next?

Finishing Club Copy properly. I’ve talked about it longer than I’ve printed anything. Cover art arguing. Recording with people who already trust the room. No interest in chasing whoever is loudest this week.

Who do you want to keep working with?

L.T. Drifta, Inlet Knight, Double-Edge. The quiet projects we’ve talked about for years. Whoever else shows up in the Pacific Northwest with a hard drive and some patience.

If this ever got big, would it change you?

Someone asked me to remake a 1970s song for a bigger-label compilation. Decent money. A day’s work. I couldn’t see my name on it, so I didn’t. Selling a lot is fine if the songs feel right. A gold record means nothing if I wouldn’t play it as a fan.

Anyone slept on?

Me. And Drifta. And whoever is still finishing a track in a basement this week.

Last words?

L.T. Drifta eats carrots.
