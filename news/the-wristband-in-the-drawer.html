The Wristband in the Drawer

You kept the first night back. You don’t wear it. The night lives in the plastic.

posted by cj archer

You cut it in July. You put it in a dish with the earrings you never take off. By January it lives in a drawer under a charger and a flyer that bled in the rain. That’s the ritual. Not a shrine. A refusal to throw away the proof that a door existed again. The rooms opened. Then they wobbled. Then Omicron made promoters send the text that starts with unfortunately. The wristband stayed. The night stayed in it.

Crate culture grades vinyl. She kept the cheap plastic. Same religion, smaller object. You don’t wear last year’s wristband to this year’s night unless you want the door to clock you as a tourist in your own memory. You keep it where you keep the other things that are not content: the mix CD, the lighter that still works, the number you never put in the phone.

If you were there, you know which night. If you weren’t, don’t borrow someone else’s plastic. The afters can be a kitchen again. The wristband is how you remember they weren’t always. Leave it in the drawer. Light something. Put the record on. That’s the whole liturgy. The door will ask for a new one when it’s time.
