Essential Listening: Homogenic

Björk. Strings and beats. The record you put on when the kitchen wants to be weather.

posted by cj archer

The crate column in this zine has been boys’ homework — Dilla is coming in December, and he’ll earn it. Here’s the record the girls already leave on. Björk, Homogenic, 1997. Iceland as a drum machine. An orchestra that doesn’t ask the kick for permission. Not a museum piece. A weather system you can put in a kitchen.

You know the temperature even if you think you don’t. “Jóga” is a landscape that still wants a body. “Bachelorette” walks in like it already paid the door. “All Is Full of Love” is the afters if the afters were honest. She made electronic music that refused to be a boy’s idea of future. The strings aren’t a costume. The beats aren’t a stunt. They’re the same girl.

I put it on when the room wants to feel bigger than the speaker. Don’t play it to prove you have taste. Play “Unravel” once without performing it. Then leave the album running. If you start moving the lights — a lamp off, a window, a jacket that finally leaves the chair — that’s the review. That’s the listening. Rosalía already took the bike this year. This is the volcano she didn’t have to name.
