Black Secret Technology

A Guy Called Gerald released the album in March 1995 on his own label, Juice Box. It entered the dance albums chart at number 8 on 1 April. The page does not give a day in March.

Black Secret Technology is a jungle album by Gerald Simpson, released in March 1995 on Juice Box, the label he ran. Wikipedia does not print a day, and this piece will not invent one. What it prints is the month, the label, the studio name Machine Room in England, a length of 77 minutes and 57 seconds, and a chart fact: the record entered the CIN Dance Albums Chart at number 8 on 1 April. The chart date is more precise than the release date. That imbalance should stay visible. A magazine can say “March” when March is all the page will support.

The album before it, 28 Gun Bad Boy, is 1992. The single “Finley’s Rainbow” is 1994. “So Many Dreams” is 1996, after the album. Gerald’s public name, A Guy Called Gerald, was already attached to “Voodoo Ray,” a house record from the previous decade. Black Secret Technology is not that record and not a continuation of it in tempo. It is a jungle album on his own label, long enough to fill a CD, issued three years after 28 Gun Bad Boy. Own-label is the structural fact. Juice Box is not a major lending its distribution to a scene. It is his. The dance-chart entry on 1 April says the shops and the chart compilers still counted it inside dance, the broad category, rather than in a separate jungle list this page does not mention.

Seventy-eight minutes is the jungle album’s ordinary ambition in the middle of the 1990s: a full disc, not an EP of dubplates. The genre tags on the page — jungle, drum and bass, ambient jungle — show the filing problem in miniature. Drum and bass and jungle are not stable opposites in 1995. They are names in the middle of a change. Ambient jungle is the qualifier for the parts that do not stay at the peak tempo. A review that picks one name and scolds the others is choosing a side in a terminology fight the record did not wait for. The record is dated March 1995. The names are the names the later page needed in order to shelve it.

The honesty about the missing day is part of the piece, not an apology stuck on the end. Volumes in this magazine are months. A record that can be placed in a month and not on a day still belongs. What it does not support is a sentence that begins “on 12 March” or any other number. If a Juice Box advertisement or a shop stamp surfaces with a day, the sentence can be revised. Until then, March 1995, Juice Box, 77:57, dance albums number 8 on 1 April. That is a complete entry.
