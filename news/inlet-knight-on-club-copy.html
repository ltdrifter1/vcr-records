We Signed Forsberg

Forsberg's Bandcamp says Cumberland. The label page says Vancouver. The records are the part that does not depend on the line.

The August 2024 note that announced Forsberg was a temperature check with a catalogue problem: it named one record and a city, and it treated the signing as a feeling. Feelings date badly. Sleeves do not. Two years on, the public record is large enough to read, and one fact in it has to be settled before the music can be.

Bandcamp, at forsberg.bandcamp.com, describes the project in a line: “House music from Cumberland, BC.” The Club Copy artist page and the roster say Vancouver. The artist-page sentence is “House out of Vancouver — locked drums, long loops, and records finished when they leave.” The roster tag is “Lo-fi House,” with the bio “Sample-driven lo-fi house from the Pacific Northwest,” and the latest release flagged there is Pitch Black, CC013. The catalogue data repeats Vancouver. Where a biography disagrees with itself, this piece follows the Bandcamp account the artist’s page actually sells from. Forsberg is house from Cumberland. The label’s own pages have not caught up. That is a correction, not a scandal. It matters because a magazine that prints the wrong town for its own artist is not documenting a scene. It is misfiling one.

The Club Copy titles, as the site lists them, are j'adore (CC007, 10 August 2026), Please Wait (17 October 2026), Run It Back (18 October 2026), Try To Love Again (18 October 2026), and Pitch Black (18 October 2026, three tracks: “Neighbourhood Spy,” “Primo,” “Pitch Black”). j'adore is the one with a production credit on the page: CJ Archer, who also writes this zine and who the about page identifies as Charlie Archer, founder of the label. A review of that single has to carry the credit in the open. Independence is not improved by leaving a producer’s name off a sleeve when the sleeve printed it. Bandcamp hosts j'adore at forsberg.bandcamp.com/album/jadore — two tracks, “don't cry” (3:27) and “j'adore” (4:51) — not at the older rainiershouse address stored in the catalogue data, which does not resolve.

The Bandcamp music list for the artist is longer than the Club Copy artist page. Alongside the titles above it includes Deeper, Outta This Mess, and a “Rose Rouge” edit. This piece will not fold those into the Club Copy story. Outta This Mess is not presented as a Club Copy record on clubcopy.ca, and the edits raise rights questions this issue is not equipped to settle. What the label page does claim is a house practice: locked drums, long loops, records finished when they leave. “Lo-fi” on the roster and “locked drums, long loops” on the artist page are not identical aesthetics. Lo-fi usually means a tolerance for dust, hiss, and the sound of a small room. Locked drums usually mean the opposite pressure: a pattern that does not wobble. A producer can do both — dusty source, strict time — and a lot of contemporary house does. The pages simply have not decided which sentence is the face. Until they do, the honest introduction is to quote both and then point at the records.

Pitch Black is the useful test of “finished when they leave.” It is billed as an album and it holds three tracks, digital, $4 CAD, released the same day as two singles, Run It Back and Try To Love Again, and a day after Please Wait. Four Club Copy titles dated 17–18 October 2026 is a burst, not a drip. A burst can mean a drawer being emptied in public, which would fit “finished when they leave” perfectly: the record is done when it is no longer being protected. It can also mean a catalogue trying to look busy. The pages do not say which. They do say each of the October singles is one track at $1.50, and that Pitch Black is the one grouped as an album. Grouping is an editorial act. Three tracks under one title ask to be heard as a set. One track at a single’s price asks to be heard as a dispatch. Forsberg’s Club Copy story, read this way, moves from a two-track single in August to a small pile in October. The August record is the one with another producer’s name on it. The October records are billed only as Forsberg’s.

Cumberland, if the Bandcamp line holds, puts Forsberg in the same town the label gives L.T. Drifta and Molly Haze, and the same town Club Copy prints on its own Bandcamp. Vancouver remains on the marketing pages. The music does not need the dispute resolved in order to be heard, but a zine does, because place is part of the claim the label makes about itself: independent electronic music from the Pacific Northwest, founded, on the about page’s data, on 12 February 2024. A house producer in that picture is not an idea of a city. He is a name, a town, a credit, and a short run of records with drums the label calls locked and loops it calls long. Start with j'adore if you want the credit visible. Start with Pitch Black if you want the latest grouping. Ignore any note, including the one this desk printed in 2024, that offers you a mood instead of a title.
