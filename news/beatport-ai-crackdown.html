The Line on AI

Beatport just told the bots to get off the decks.

Label noteSep 5, 2026

CJ Archer

Beatport just told the bots to get off the decks.

The store where most working DJs still buy their sets announced it's banning tracks that are fully or mostly made by AI. Anything built with AI assistance can stay, as long as a human is still doing the actual work — those get a quiet tag so the curation team knows what it's looking at. Anything that's basically a machine's idea of a record gets pulled, and the rights holder gets a note explaining why.

The timing isn't random. Streaming platforms say AI tracks now make up something like 44% of everything uploaded every day — tens of thousands of songs — while actually accounting for barely 1 to 3% of real listening. Most of it is spam dressed up as music, chasing royalty pools that are supposed to be for people who made something. TIDAL stopped paying out on fully AI-generated tracks back in June. Deezer now strips most of the plays on flagged AI tracks out as fraud before it even runs the payout math. Apple's rolling out its own "Made With AI" label, and Spotify's doing something similar this month.

Beatport ran the numbers on its own users before making the call: 77% said they'd rather support a human artist, 60% said they'd flat-out skip an AI track. Only 8% didn't care either way. That's not a branding decision. That's just what the room wants.

For a small label, this actually matters. Beatport charts are still how a lot of DJs find new music, and a flood of machine-made noise makes it harder for an actual record to surface. Our own Riscape landed a Beatport No. 1 on the Trance chart this year with a remix — the kind of moment that means something because a person built it, track by track, at 3am, and other people noticed. That's the whole point of a record store, and it's good to see one draw a line.
