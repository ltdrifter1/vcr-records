Riscape: Heads Down

Athens, a conservatory, two Club Copy titles, and a chart claim the label bio states and this piece will not pretend to have rechecked.

Riscape is the Club Copy artist whose biography is long enough to check and whose records on the label are few enough to list. The artist page places him in Athens. It says he studied piano at Chalandri’s National Conservatory, learned production at SAE Athens and Grey Studios, and began releasing music in 2020. From 2021 he was on Mystery Freedom, a Dutch imprint, with EPs including Unity and Two x S. The roster line is shorter: “Melodic songwriting meets peak-time energy,” tagged melodic house. The page’s own summary is the same pairing, classical training on one side and clubs on the other. That pairing is a cliché until a record shows which side is load-bearing. On the evidence of the credits, both sides signed the work.

The chart line has to be handled as a quotation from the bio, not as a trophy this desk weighed. In 2026, the page says, he reached number one on Beatport’s Trance (Main Floor) Top 100 New Releases with a remix of “Synth Magic” by Alphed Le Cornett and JARR Head. Third-party listings of the remix exist; the ranking is the biography’s. A magazine that prints “Beatport number one” as a bare fact, because its own artist page said so, is doing publicity. A magazine that prints “his label bio says he had a Beatport number one” is doing the job. The difference is the whole of editorial independence when the subject pays the hosting.

What Club Copy itself has released is easier. You Are (Love), CC004, 18 September 2026: four tracks, 15:02, “You Are (Love),” “Make It Right,” “Action,” and “Days Like This” on the Bandcamp sleeve. Written by Ioannis Rigas, produced by Riscape, for Club Copy and Copy House Publishing. His Bandcamp note asks for vintage and contemporary at once, and for homage to classic house structures. Tell Me, CC009, 10 October 2026: “My Whole Life,” “Tell Me Something,” “Tell Me Nothing,” digital $4.99, with a 24-bit download mentioned on the page. Three weeks between an EP and a second EP is a working pace. It is not a rebrand. The titles on both records speak in ordinary sentences. Love, repair, a kind of day, a whole life, tell me something, tell me nothing. Melodic house, as a market category, often hides that plainness under a longer abstract title. These titles decline the abstraction.

The conservatory detail is the one that needs translating for a reader who does not care where a producer went to school. Chalandri is a neighborhood in northern Athens. A national conservatory, in this context, means years of piano as a discipline: repertoire, exams, the idea that a note has a right place before it has a mood. SAE and Grey Studios are the later, vocational half — rooms where electronic music is taught as a craft with software and signal flow. Plenty of dance producers have one half of that story. The page insists on both, and then the Club Copy credit splits “written by” from “produced by” under two names for the same person. Ioannis Rigas writes. Riscape produces. The pseudonym is the club name. The legal-looking name is the composing name. That is a small, old-fashioned bit of seriousness, and it matches the Bandcamp note’s talk of homage better than a single brand name would.

Off the label, the page points to Riscape Reality Radio, a mix series of records he is listening to plus his own, and to support from playlists and DJs it does not list title by title. Support quotes without the supporter’s sentence are not very useful, so they stay in the bio. The useful off-label fact is the mix series, because it rhymes with something this magazine can see elsewhere on the roster. L.T. Drifta’s public work is also split between priced records and a long mixes list. Riscape’s split is between a Dutch label, a trance remix, a radio show, and two Club Copy EPs. A small imprint in British Columbia releasing an Athens producer is not a scene merging. It is a catalogue deciding that melodic house belongs next to Cumberland jungle-and-beats and Cumberland-or-Vancouver house, depending on which Forsberg page you believe. Geography here is the point of the signing, and it does not require a metaphor about distance. The records arrived by the ordinary digital route. The piano study happened in Athens. The catalogue number is CC004 and then CC009.

Earlier Night Shift copy said the label signed him in 2026, and other copy has said August, and a 2022 interview on this site already speaks of him as if he were around. Those sentences do not agree, so this piece does not pick a signing date. It picks the release dates, which do agree. 18 September 2026, then 10 October 2026. Four songs, then three. A writer’s name and a producer’s name. A chart claim left inside quotation marks. That is a sturdier profile than a packed floor at three in the morning, which no page of ours has documented with a room, a night, or a witness. The music is the part we can point to. The rest can wait for a night that actually gets written down.
