Mezzanine

Massive Attack, 1998. I put it on at the sink after last call, when the kitchen still wants weather and nobody is brave enough for something cheerful.

1998virginlp

posted by cj archer · Oct 31, 2026

October 31 can keep the fake blood. I put Mezzanine on when the apartment needs to feel like a wet street and nobody is willing to go back outside. Massive Attack, 1998, Virgin: Bristol bass arriving the way weather arrives, a voice that sounds like it already left and then came back because the night was not finished arguing with itself. The 4 a.m. record. You can stand in it.

The boys filed it under trip-hop and moved to the next assignment, which is how some people survive beauty without having to feel it. I file it under the hour when the kitchen is still open and nobody is brave enough for something cheerful. “Teardrop” is the famous one. It still works if you let Elizabeth Fraser sit in the dark instead of singing along like you are auditioning for your own memory. “Inertia Creeps” is the long dark. “Angel” is Horace Andy telling you the night has a pulse whether you dance or not. The bass does not care about your costume.

I have walked home to this, wet collar, keys already cold. I have skipped the walk and put it on anyway, last night’s jersey, dishes making their small honest noise in a sink that still has someone else’s glass. Both counted. Love Deluxe is the apartment with people still in it, warm enough that conversation survives. This is the apartment after they leave, when you are not ready to be kind about the quiet. It does not ask you to feel better. It asks you to stay with what you already feel until the feeling finishes its sentence.

I do the dishes through “Group Four” and I do not text. The coat can stay on the chair if it wants; I am not performing hope for furniture. By the time the side runs out, Bristol has done what Cascadia was too tired to do, and the kitchen has weather again. Candy can wait on the stoop. The bass already came in.
