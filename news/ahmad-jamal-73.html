Essential Listening: Ahmad Jamal '73

CJ Archer

Most people meet Ahmad Jamal through the acoustic stuff — space, touch, the trio that made Miles tell his band to go study. Fair. Dig a little further and you hit Ahmad Jamal '73: all Fender Rhodes, Chicago production with Richard Evans, covers that float instead of shout. War, Stevie, then originals that still turn up in beatmaker crates decades later.

Cool thing most write-ups skip: the Rhodes Jamal leaned on in that era wasn’t some carefully shopped studio flex. It was a gift from someone in Switzerland. He took an instrument that arrived as a personal gesture and made a whole record speak electric — not as a novelty, as another kind of touch. Same ear for space. Different voltage.

He never liked being boxed as a “jazz” pianist anyway. Called it American classical music. Listen to '73 with that in mind and the Rhodes makes sense: not jumping trends, just widening the instrument until the groove had room to breathe. Peace at Last still feels like someone leaving the door open on purpose.

If your digs lean sample-heavy, this is one of those LPs that teaches patience — lush without getting soft, funky without rushing the pocket. Put it on after the acoustic classics. Notice how the same pianist disappears into electricity and still sounds like himself.
