Inlet Knight

Rosco’s first full statement: eleven tracks, a cassette you can hold, and a boat photographed on actual water.

I have missed my exit on purpose to this record. More than once. Eleven tracks, late, the dashboard doing that cheap glow it does on the Second Narrows when you were supposed to get off two ramps ago and you did not, because Inlet Knight was still in the middle of a thought.

Rosco wrote and performed it. I produced it. We dubbed a cassette because some of these hours still want to be held, CC005, digital eight dollars, tape twenty, thirty-six shells in the first run. The artwork is a boat on actual water, not a moodboard of water. Vancouver does that to you. You can live here a decade and still not be done looking at it. I put this on when the rain starts and I decide I am not going out after all, which is its own night, kettle, wet glass, no flyer required.

“Revive Him” opens like someone checking a pulse. “Sunday Afternoon” is the window weather. “3AM” is the hour in the title and in the drums. “Is It Cool?” is the last question before you admit you are staying. j'adore was the car door, two tracks, fisheye blue, a passenger you had not figured out. This is the whole crossing. I do not DJ it. I let the boat go from Revive to Cool and I take the long way home even when I am already home.
