No Good (Start the Dance)

The Prodigy released the single on 16 May 1994. The album it is on, Music for the Jilted Generation, came out on 4 July, on XL.

Music for the Jilted Generation, the Prodigy’s second album, is a July release: 4 July 1994, XL, recorded at Strongroom in London and Earthbound in Braintree, Essex, produced by Liam Howlett and Neil McLellan, 78 minutes and 7 seconds. The single “No Good (Start the Dance)” is dated 16 May 1994. “One Love” is 4 October 1993. “Voodoo People” is 12 September 1994. “Poison” is 6 March 1995. The album sits in the middle of a singles campaign that runs from the autumn before to the spring after. May is the month of the single that carries the dance instruction in its title. July is the month of the album. They are separated here so the single can be a single.

The genre list on the album page — breakbeat hardcore, hardcore techno, rave — describes a group that had already released Experience in 1992 and had not yet released The Fat of the Land, which XL dates to 30 June 1997. May 1994 is between those two albums, closer to the second than to the third. “No Good” is how the second album announced itself while the album was still six weeks away. A single with a parenthesis for a command is a pop object. The album it feeds is filed, by the later page, under rave and hardcore as well as breakbeat. The command and the genre list do not have to match. The single can address a dance floor in the title while the album page describes a broader hardcore record. Titles are advertising. Genre tags are filing. Both are public, and they serve different readers.

Essex and London are the album’s studios, and this piece borrows them for the single only as the places the album was made, not as a claim that the single was cut in one room or the other. Braintree is a specific town in the studio credit, which is more useful than “the provinces.” Strongroom is a specific London room other records in this run also pass through — Underworld’s Dubnobasswithmyheadman names it too, in January 1994. A studio used by more than one group in the same year is a piece of infrastructure. It is not a scene. Scenes are what people claim after the bookings.

Sixteen May is the date to keep. The parenthesis in the title is the rest of the public text this piece needs. Start the dance is an instruction the song gives itself. A magazine does not have to repeat the instruction as advice to a reader. The reader is not being sent onto a floor. The reader is being shown a release date, a label, two studios, and the six-week gap between a single and the album that completed the thought. July’s piece on the album can start from the album. May’s piece stops at the single, which is where May stops.
