The Coat on the Chair

In June 2021 the going-out top stayed on and the coat never left the kitchen chair — no cloakroom to prove you’d meant to leave, only the fit as evidence.

posted by cj archer · Jun 4, 2021

June 2021 and the night is linoleum again. Patio rumour floating through the chat like weather that might not arrive, like permission that might get revoked before the Uber even thinks about moving. Going-out top under a hoodie because hope is a layer. Platforms that never saw a curb, practiced on the same kitchen floor that has been the club for too many months, height without destination. You got dressed like the door might exist. The coat went on the chair because there was no cloakroom and you were not taking it to the bedroom like a civilian admitting the night was cancelled. Leaving it crumpled there was a kind of faith. The floor is the photograph. The kitchen is the edit. Cute as armor did not start on TikTok. It started when the only witnesses were the group chat and the girl on the other bunk, zoomed in close enough to see the lip and still kind enough not to screenshot the soft parts.

In 1999 you left the jacket with a stranger and prayed the ticket stub would still mean something at 3 a.m., that the face at the door would remember you. In 2003 the camera phone was still a rumour and the lip gloss lived in the pocket of low-rise everything, migrating onto whatever it touched until the night was sticky with evidence. This year the stall is your own bathroom and the light is still criminal, still honest in a way the living room isn’t. You still redraw the lip between tracks. You still decide if you’re staying — except staying means staying, because leaving only takes you as far as the hallway and the hallway only takes you back. The night is the apartment. The fit is the proof you intended to leave, even when the street isn’t open for that intention yet, even when the coat has nowhere to check itself except the back of a chair that has learned to wait.

Keep the coat on the chair. Don’t fold it. Folding means you admitted nobody was coming, that the rumour of a patio was just a rumour and the evening should put itself away neatly like a good citizen. If she shows up in the thing she bought in March 2020 — the dress that waited through a year of cancelled plans, tags maybe still half-attached in spirit — you tell her. That’s the whole review. The xerox of the evening is already on the collar, a soft crease where hope sat down and refused to hang itself up. The street can wait. The chair can hold the coat until the city remembers how to take it, and until then the going-out top stays on like a promise you made to yourself in the mirror.
