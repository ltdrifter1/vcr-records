The Flyer on the Fridge

The boys grade vinyl. She kept the xerox that actually smelled like the room.

posted by cj archer

Crate culture likes to pretend memory is a 12-inch you can grade. Most of the nights I still think about live on cheap paper. Fridge magnet. Wallet. Slipped in a book nobody finished. A stub that only says you were there, and the ink already wanted to leave.

Diggers talk first pressings. The flyer was the first pressing of the night. Xerox, a neon that shouldn’t exist, a font that should be illegal. You took it because the night might not happen again, and because somebody’s handwriting was on the back. Forty-five dollars is a record. The paper that still smells like the room is also a record.

When some shop tries to sell your youth back to you as a drop, look at the fridge. The magnet held. The paper yellowed on purpose. That’s an archive. The Discogs alerts can stay with the boys. She’s got last summer next to the takeout menu, which is also a log of who came over after.

If you still have the handbill, don’t laminate it. Let it curl. The curl is how you know you lived it, not that you filed it.
