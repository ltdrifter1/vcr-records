She Kept the Flyer

Crate culture grades vinyl. She kept the cheap paper.

posted by cj archer

Crate culture likes to pretend memory is a 12-inch you can grade. The girls kept the cheap paper. Fridge magnet. Wallet. The one slipped in a book you never finished. A ticket stub that says nothing except you were there and the ink already wanted to leave.

Diggers talk about first pressings. The flyer was the first pressing of the night. Xerox, neon, a font that should be illegal. You took it because the night might not happen again and because somebody’s handwriting was on the back. Collecting is not only the object that costs forty-five dollars. Collecting is the thing that still smells like the room.

When the algorithm tries to sell your youth back to you as a drop, look at the fridge. The magnet held. The paper yellowed on purpose. That’s an archive. That’s not a raid. The boys can keep the Discogs alerts. She already has the night on the kitchen door, next to the takeout menu, which is also a record of who came over after.

If you still have last summer’s handbill, don’t laminate it. Let it curl. The curl is the proof you lived it, not that you filed it.
