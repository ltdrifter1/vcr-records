Hymn of the Big Wheel

Massive Attack issued the single, backed with “Be Thankful for What You’ve Got,” on 10 February 1992. Blue Lines was already ten months old.

The last single on the Blue Lines list is the one that falls outside the album’s year. “Hymn of the Big Wheel,” with “Be Thankful for What You’ve Got,” is dated 10 February 1992. The album had been out since 8 April 1991. “Unfinished Sympathy” had been a single since 11 February 1991, almost exactly a year earlier. The February symmetry is accidental and still visible: one single a year, on the 10th and the 11th. Accident is not a theory. It is a reason to look twice at a discography before you smooth it into an era.

By February 1992 the album was no longer news. A single from it, this late, is a catalogue decision. Labels keep working a record when the record is still selling, or when a song has not had its own object yet. “Be Thankful for What You’ve Got” is a cover, William DeVaughn’s song, and the credit structure of the single puts it on the other side. A B-side that is itself famous is a way of spending catalogue attention twice. This piece will not describe either recording bar by bar. It will note that the February 1992 release is how the album continued to meet a shop after the debut year had closed.

Horace Andy, named on the album page among the singers, is the voice associated with “Hymn of the Big Wheel” in the ordinary telling of the record. The album page in front of this desk names him as a contributor and does not isolate that song in the sentence this piece has. So the sentence stops at the contributor credit. Andy is on Blue Lines. The single is dated. The link between his name and this particular title is the common account, and a careful page says the common account is common. The date does not depend on it.

A volume that also contains the February 1991 single and, in April, the album, can hold this third date without repeating the same essay. The 1992 single is the afterlife of the debut. Debut albums that matter keep issuing paper after the year of release. The paper here is a 10 February single, a cover on the reverse, and a group that had already been to Coach House in Bristol and to Abbey Road. The month files it. The album does not need to be reviewed again for the single to have a place.
