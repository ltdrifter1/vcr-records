8 August 1994

Snivilisation is Orbital’s third studio album, released 8 August 1994 on FFRR. Paul and Phil Hartnoll are the duo. The Wikipedia running time is 75 minutes and 4 seconds. Recording is dated 1993–94. The genres listed are techno and ambient techno. The album before it, titled Orbital, is 1993. The album after it, In Sides, is dated 29 April 1996 on its own page, issued in the UK on Internal, with a length of 72 minutes and 2 seconds, recorded at The Strongroom in London. The single from In Sides, “The Box,” is 15 April 1996, two weeks before that album. Snivilisation’s own page does not itemise its singles in the extract this desk is using beyond the fact of the album date. This piece will not add a singles list it has not copied. It will use the next album’s date, which is copied, as the thing that happened after.

Seventy-five minutes in August 1994 sits beside the Prodigy’s 78 minutes in July and Underworld’s 73 minutes in January. British electronic albums in that year were long. They were using the CD’s capacity as the normal size of a statement. FFRR, XL, and Junior Boy’s Own were all willing to release that size. Orbital’s version is the August one. Ambient techno, as a tag, distinguishes it from the hardcore list on the Prodigy page without requiring a feud. Tags are how shops and later writers sort a season that was, at the time, simply full of long electronic records. The sorting can be acknowledged and not turned into teams.

The Hartnolls, and the room that comes next

The producer credit on In Sides is Paul and Phil Hartnoll, which confirms the duo as the producers of the follow-up and is the best sourced description of who makes Orbital records. Snivilisation’s page, in the material at hand, names them by naming the group and the producer field in the usual way these infoboxes work: the group produced the group’s album. If a different engineer is on the sleeve, this page has not cited the sleeve and will not guess. What it will say is that the next album was made at Strongroom, the London studio already named on Underworld’s January 1994 album and on the Prodigy’s July 1994 album. By April 1996, Strongroom had held at least three of the records this magazine’s year is built from. That is a workplace with a particular client list, not a mystical room. August 1994’s album is not given Strongroom in the sentences this desk copied. The next one is. The distinction stays.

A third album is past the debut’s excuses. Orbital had released a self-titled record in 1993 and, before that, the records that made the name on 12-inch. Snivilisation is the album on which the group were no longer introducing the project. Seventy-five minutes of techno and ambient techno, on a major-associated label, FFRR, in August, is a mid-career object that happens to sit early in a wider history. Mid-career is the right tempo for the criticism. The record does not have to be a beginning. Beginnings are over-rewarded. A third album that holds a CD’s length of composed electronic pieces, and is followed twenty months later by another of similar length, is a practice. Practices are what an archive is for.

August in a year of long records

The Prodigy’s second album was four weeks old when Snivilisation came out. A Guy Called Gerald’s Black Secret Technology would be dated the following March, with no day attached. Leftfield’s Leftism would be the following January. 1994’s August is not the peak of that sequence. It is a point in it. Orbital’s contribution to the point is a title, a length, a label, and a duo. The title is a spelling joke on civilisation. This piece will not unpack the joke into a theory of society. Jokes on sleeves are allowed to remain jokes. The length and the label are the operable facts. FFRR could ship 75 minutes of techno in August and the group could be back with 72 minutes in April 1996, recorded at a studio their peers had already used. That is a portrait of a working duo, which is more durable than a portrait of a moment.

In May 1997, In Sides was reissued in the UK with the single “The Saint” added. The vinyl reissue was a four-LP box. The reissue is not Snivilisation, and it is mentioned only to show that Orbital’s album catalogue, like Boards of Canada’s and Aphex Twin’s, exists in editions. The 1994 album’s 75:04 should not be silently replaced by a later configuration if one exists. This page has not been shown a later configuration of Snivilisation and will not imply one. The 8 August 1994 release is the object. FFRR is the label. The Hartnolls are the group. The next date to remember is 29 April 1996, and it belongs to a different album.

A cover story without a photograph is still a cover story. This volume’s other pieces have their own images or their own silence. Snivilisation does not get a picture it has not licensed. It gets the date, the label, the length, and the company of the long British electronic albums issued around it, each on its own label, none of them reduced to a caption. August 1994 is late summer on a calendar and early in the discography of album-form techno. Orbital were already on album three. The summer was not waiting for them. They were already at work.
