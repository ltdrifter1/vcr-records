Tomorrow’s Harvest

Boards of Canada’s fourth studio album is listed as 5 June 2013, on Warp. The UK peak in the discography is 7. The debut, fifteen years earlier, had peaked at 193.

Tomorrow’s Harvest is the fourth studio album in the Boards of Canada discography, released 5 June 2013 on Warp, as CD, vinyl, and digital download. The UK peak in that table is 7. Australia is 24. The United States figure is 13. Ireland is 7. The debut, Music Has the Right to Children, is listed at UK 193 in April 1998. Geogaddi, February 2002, is at 21. The fourth album arriving at 7 is the chart’s version of a group becoming a guaranteed albums-chart act without this page needing to describe a single bar of the music. Peaks are not quality. They are a record of how many people showed up in the first weeks. By 2013, more people showed up.

Warp is the only label in the cell. The Skam and Matador credits of the debut are gone from this row, as they were gone from Geogaddi’s row. The label relationship simplified. The duo’s description at the top of the page — Scottish electronic group — does not get a new studio line here. Hexagon Sun stays on the 1998 album’s own page. This essay will not carry the studio forward on loyalty. If the fourth album was made there, a different citation can say so. The discography row says Warp, 5 June 2013, and a UK peak of 7.

June 2013 is a long way from June 1997, when New Forms and The Fat of the Land came out. The month is the filing system, not a claim of kinship. What the 2013 row is doing in this volume is giving the Scottish duo’s later catalogue a month of its own, instead of leaving every Boards of Canada fact piled on the 1998 debut. A magazine that only ever writes about the first album has a souvenir, not a discography. The fourth album’s peak is the fact that makes the souvenir look incomplete. People were still buying the name, on vinyl and CD and download, fifteen years after a record that had barely charted.

The singles table on the same page lists “Reach for the Dead” as a digital download connected to Tomorrow’s Harvest, and a 2013 12-inch of untitled tracks as a non-album single. The untitled 12-inch is mentioned only to note that the group were still issuing records without titles in the year of a titled album. Untitled, for this duo and for Aphex Twin’s Volume II, is a design habit, not an accident of a missing booklet. The album itself has a title. The extra 12-inch, that year, did not need one. June holds the album. The 12-inch can stay a sentence.
