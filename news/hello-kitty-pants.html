The Pants Stay On

Nine people signed a petition to ban Hello Kitty pajama pants at raves. Glasgow is printing the cat on the flyer anyway.

posted by cj archer

I saw the petition before I saw the pants, which is the part that got me. Someone named Samantha Scott in the UK opened Change.org on February 6 and decided the emergency in rave culture was Hello Kitty pajama pants. Mass-produced sleepwear, she wrote. “Not self-expression, it’s laziness.” The line that got screenshotted into every group chat: those pants say you got dressed in the dark and called it a fit.

EDM.com counted the signatures. Nine. It has been sitting there since winter. I asked around. Nobody I know who actually goes out had signed it, posted a door policy about it, or even finished reading it. The fashion police arrived with a Google Form and the chat treated it like a meme for about forty minutes.

I keep thinking about a girl I saw last summer in pink drawstrings, a black baby tee, and a lip gloss she reapplied off someone’s phone flashlight. She looked like she lived there. Half the room was in something you could sleep in. Baggy jorts. An oversized tee. Somebody’s older cousin is always waiting to tell you that’s not how it used to look. Fine. It used to look like furry boots and kandi stacked to the elbow and a pacifier you swore was ironic. Cute as armor is not a new idea. You don’t owe a strobe a corset because 2003 had a dress code.

They say “standards” when they mean they don’t like who looks comfortable. “Raves are not sleepovers.” Some of the best nights of my life ended in a kitchen in socks. The same girls who used to safety-pin a pacifier to a backpack are tying a bow on the drawstring and walking in anyway. That isn’t a crisis. That’s the culture doing what it always does.

Glasgow is throwing a Hello Kitty rave this spring — hardcore, hardstyle, techno — and the flyer tells you to wear the PJs and not get judged. Meet-and-greet with the cat. One petition wants the pants gone. The rooms that actually open are printing her on the paper.

If you’re twenty-four in pink drawstrings under a laser, you are not the problem. Wear them. Nine people on the internet are not the door.
