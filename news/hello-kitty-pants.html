The Pants Stay On

Nine signatures. A fake crisis. The girls already decided.

posted by cj archer

Someone in the UK opened a Change.org tab and decided the real threat to rave culture was Hello Kitty pajama pants. Samantha Scott, February 6. The petition calls it a crisis: people showing up in mass-produced sleepwear, “not self-expression, it’s laziness.” The line that went around: Hello Kitty pants say you got dressed in the dark and called it a fit.

EDM.com counted the signatures. Nine. The petition has been sitting there since winter and the floor did not flinch. No club posted a door policy. No promoter sent a memo. The fashion police arrived with a Google Form and nobody RSVP’d.

This is always how the argument goes. Millennials remember when the uniform was furry boots, kandi stacked to the elbow, a pacifier you swore was ironic. Then the next room showed up in oversized tees, baggy jorts, the Target pajama aisle worn like a dare. Comfort as rebellion is not new. Cute as armor is not new. You don’t owe the strobe a corset because somebody’s older cousin had a dress code in 2003.

Standards is the word they use when they mean they don’t like who looks like they belong. “Raves are not sleepovers.” Some of the best nights of my life ended in a kitchen in socks. The after was always the point. Sleepwear on the floor is a lineage — PLUR with a barcode, the same girls who used to safety-pin a pacifier to a backpack now tying a pink bow on drawstrings and getting through the door anyway.

The culture is not confused. Glasgow is throwing a Hello Kitty rave this spring that tells you to wear the PJs and not get judged. Hardcore, hardstyle, techno. Meet-and-greet with the cat. One petition wants the pants gone. The actual rooms are printing the cat on the flyer.

If you’re twenty-four in pink drawstrings under a laser, you are not the problem. Wear the pants. The petition cannot touch a door. The fit was never theirs to keep.
