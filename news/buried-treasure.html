Buried Treasure

Club Copy

CJ Archer keeps three piles. Records for listening. Records for sampling. Records for remembering where a night went. Buried treasure, for him, is whatever survives the sort — the side you flip twice, the break that still hits in the rain on the way home.

He came up buying house twelves and jungle white labels next to funk and soul, then stopped pretending those piles were separate. Mid-2000s Vancouver: dance music, breaks, and a better ear for where the drums actually came from. The crates got deeper. The sessions got quieter. The name stuck — plain enough to work, pointed enough to aim.

Club Copy grew out of that same habit. Not a feed flex. A room with a hard drive and patience. Archer produces across the catalogue and runs the label side with the same pickiness he brings to artwork: if he wouldn’t put his name on it as a fan, it doesn’t ship. Quality of listener over quantity. No dollar-bin future if he can help it.

Ask him about influences and he’ll point you back into the bins. Ask him who’s heating the room and he’ll name the people already inside it — L.T. Drifta, Inlet Knight, Double-Edge — different tempos, same honesty. The Northwest doesn’t need a slogan. It needs records that hold.

That’s the dig. Pull it up. Press it. See who stays.
