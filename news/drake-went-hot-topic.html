Drake Went Hot Topic and the Internet Lost Its Mind

Pinkchyu walked him into the mall, spiked the choker, and set Princess to a private Medieval Times. Rollout genius or midlife meme. The timeline cannot pick one.

format
Essay
date
Sep 19, 2026
author
CJ Archer
artists
Drake
words
803 words · 3 min

There is a version of fame that still ends in a fitting room under fluorescent light, and this week Drake walked straight into it.

Inside FOMO, his self-funded hour-long film that premiered Tuesday, September 15, a stretch set to Princess from Maid of Honour sends him hand in hand through a mall with Pinkchyu, the Texas creator who won his August 20 v 1 dating stream as the Goth Baddie. They stop at Hot Topic. She dresses him. He comes out all black: graphic tees, chains, baggy trousers, chunky boots, and a spiked choker that the internet immediately treated like a punchline and a sacrament at the same time. Night park walk. Private Medieval Times. The fit stays on. Complex, TMZ, Fashion Times, and HotNewHipHop all walked the same route through the clip, which is how you know the segment did what it was built to do.

Night Shift is not here to decide whether a thirty-nine-year-old megastar looks good in mall emo. Night Shift is here for the aftercare of the moment: what happens when a rollout stops pretending it lives only on the charts and starts living in the same stores where somebody once bought their first band tee and a bottle of eyeliner that never quite matched the lighting at home.

Rewind one month. Kick stream. Twenty people. Pinkchyu, real name Lin Lamar according to Fashion Times, twenty-three, cosplayer and OnlyFans model with a following already measured in the millions before Drake showed up. She wins. She does not take a fixed cash prize. She names a house for her retired mother. Drake agrees on air. Somewhere in the middle of that circus he looks at her and says she can ask him to do anything. She tests him. Would you bark for me. He barks. Multiple times. The clip does what clips do. Hypefresh, via Fashion Times, put her X growth after the stream near half a million. That is not a footnote. That is the creator economy doing arithmetic in public.

TMZ had them again in Toronto with Casa Loma rented out, sushi, drinks, the castle mostly empty for two people who somehow still felt like a bit. Then FOMO put the bit on a bigger stage. HotNewHipHop noted the livestream pulled tens of thousands live, previewed unreleased music (Don Toliver and Yeat among the names floating in coverage), and that Drake flagged a 2027 tour as obviously coming. The film vanished from YouTube after the stream, then returned in full on Thursday with at least one edit (Ella Langley gone from the Choosin Texas stretch, per HotNewHipHop). The machine keeps moving even when the file does.

The timeline split on schedule. One camp called the fitting-room exit peak mall boyfriend energy and meant it as affection. Another pointed at the Funko Pop wall and asked who still thinks Hot Topic is the edge of anything. Age jokes. Kendrick jokes. AI jokes because the polish looked too clean. Fans insisting he is just having fun. Fashion Times framed the whole thing as cross-audience bait: hip-hop kids, millennial mall-emo muscle memory, Gen Z creator watchers all hitting the same share button. Night Shift will not invent which camp is correct. Night Shift will say the argument is the product.

What lands for a Club Copy reader is simpler and older than Drake’s PR calendar. The night has always been half music and half costume. Platforms. Compacts. A stall light that lies. A bag that holds nothing useful and everything you need. Hot Topic is a joke until you remember how many people learned to build a self out of studs and black denim under a mall roof that smelled like pretzels and ambition. Watching a global rapper submit to that liturgy, spiked choker and all, is either embarrassing or holy depending on how much you still believe the fit can change the weather. Both readings can sit in the same booth.

Pinkchyu posted the couple energy with a black heart. Drake kept the look through Medieval Times like he meant the bit. Princess keeps playing under it. FOMO keeps the conversation warm while the next album rumour does its usual stretch. You do not have to like any of that to recognise the shape: fame borrowing the mall’s oldest trick, which is letting somebody else pick the outfit and daring the room to clock you for it.

Stream the segment if you want the primary text. Argue about the choker if you need a hobby. Just do not pretend this is only about Drake being weird. It is about how a rollout now runs through creator-economy chaos, eyeliner energy, and a store that still sells the dream of being seen as someone else for one fluorescent hour. The internet lost its mind on cue. That was never an accident.
