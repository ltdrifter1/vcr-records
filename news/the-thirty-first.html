The Thirty-First

Frankie Knuckles died in Chicago on 31 March 2014, aged 59. He was born Francis Warren Nicholls Jr. in New York on 18 January 1955. The obituaries had to use both cities.

Francis Warren Nicholls Jr. was born in New York City on 18 January 1955 and died in Chicago on 31 March 2014, aged 59. The Wikipedia summary of the death says complications from diabetes. CNN’s obituary, filed 3 April 2014, uses the same date and the same age, and calls him a producer, remixer, and house-music pioneer. The page also records a 1997 Grammy for Remixer of the Year, non-classical, and the phrase he was often called: the godfather of house music. This piece will use the phrase as a phrase people used, not as a title this magazine is handing out.

The medical details on the encyclopedia page are more specific than a eulogy usually is, and they are part of the public record. In the mid-2000s he developed type 2 diabetes. After breaking his foot snowboarding he developed osteomyelitis, declined to take time off, and had the foot amputated. He died in Chicago. A music magazine does not need to linger on the illness to refuse the version of the story in which a pioneer simply vanishes. He died of a named condition, in a named city, on a named day. Chicago is the city the obituaries attach to the death. New York is the city attached to the birth. House music’s origin story, on the same page, is Chicago in the early 1980s, after he had come up as a DJ from the middle of the 1970s. The two cities are both true. Collapsing them into one skyline is how a short obituary gets its picture. The dates keep them apart.

January and March both have a claim on the name, which is why a January volume in this run does not also carry this piece. He was born on 18 January. The Official Charts week that put a Chicago house instrumental at number one in Britain, Steve “Silk” Hurley’s “Jack Your Body,” falls in that same month in 1987, thirty-two years after the birth date. The death is 31 March 2014. A monthly magazine files the death in March because that is the event with a closing date. The birth is context, printed here so the January date is not lost, and left in January’s other pages only where those pages are about January releases.

What the obituary cannot do is replay a set. No tracklist of the Warehouse, or of the Power Plant, is in the sentences this piece is willing to treat as sourced from the pages just cited. The pages support a role: DJ, producer, remixer, a Grammy for remixing, a public nickname, two cities, a cause of death. Remixer of the Year is an award for work on other people’s records. That is a clue to the practice. House, in his case, was not only a night. It was a way of rebuilding songs that already existed, and the Grammy category was willing to treat that rebuilding as authorship. The thirty-first of March is the date the rebuilding stopped. The records did not.

Sources

  • Wikipedia: birth 18 January 1955, death 31 March 2014, Chicago, Grammy, diabetes.
  • CNN, 3 April 2014: obituary confirming the date and the age.
