Selling Your Digs Back to You

Someone built a store out of other people’s crates.

posted by cj archer

The old dream: every private press, every Greek disco 45, every thing you spent a decade hunting — the one you remember by the sleeve, not the Discogs number — available at checkout.

Resident Advisor published it this week: an online shop called Revibed accused of selling unlicensed vinyl rips without clearing the masters. The pitch was crate culture as a service — rare records, digitised, yours in a click. The catch, if you still believe in one, is that the people who pressed those records didn’t get asked. A shop built on somebody else’s collection is not a dig. That’s a raid.

We’ve all lived the mp3-blog years. We all downloaded the rip of the 12" that never got repressed. That was a bedroom economy with no checkout. This is a storefront. Different temperature. The second you put a price on an uncleared transfer, you’re not archiving the underground. You’re metering it.

We run a label that still believes in the physical object and the person who found it. Flip until something holds. If the holding is just a WAV of a record you never paid for, keep walking. The crate was never supposed to be a SKU.
