Girl EDM Took the Trophy

Ninajirachi walked into the ARIAs with eight nominations and left with three wins. The computer girl was never a novelty.

posted by cj archer

Ninajirachi arrived at the 2025 ARIAs with eight nominations — the most of anyone in the room — and left holding three: Best Solo Artist, the Michael Gudinski Breakthrough Artist award and Best Independent Release. Not Best Dance Act, tucked politely at the genre table. Best solo artist. Nina Wilson, twenty-six, from the Central Coast, beat the old industry habit of treating the woman behind the laptop like somebody’s opening set.

Her debut album, I Love My Computer, came out through NLV Records in August. The title is not retro-futurist decoration. Wilson has talked about finding music through YouTube tutorials as a kid, producing through her teens and spending more time with her machine than almost anybody. The record turns that intimacy into high-gloss, high-speed feeling: 2010s EDM remembered by someone who survived the internet that sold it to us.

“Girl EDM” began as a useful little provocation — cute enough to fit in a caption, sharp enough to expose the boys’ club hiding inside the word EDM. Then the supposed microgenre won the Australian Music Prize, triple j’s Album of the Year and three ARIAs. The joke kept collecting hardware. The men who say women only get booked for optics may now direct complaints to the trophy cabinet.

The best line of the night came inside the Breakthrough win: to receive one in 2025 was, she said, “really funny and awesome.” She had been working for years. That is usually what “overnight” means when the producer is a woman: a decade of files nobody important opened until everybody did at once.

Put on “Fuck My Computer” when the screen has taken too much and somehow still knows you best. This was not the underground being rescued by an awards show. It was the awards show finally noticing where the future had been saving its sessions.
