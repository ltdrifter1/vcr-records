Unfinished Sympathy

The single came out on 11 February 1991. Blue Lines, the album it is on, came out on 8 April. The song was public before the record that now contains it.

Blue Lines is dated 8 April 1991, Wild Bunch and Virgin, recorded at Coach House in Bristol and at Eastcote, Cherry Bear, Abbey Road, and Hot Nights in London. The single list on the same Wikipedia page puts “Unfinished Sympathy” on 11 February 1991. “Daydreaming” is earlier still, 15 October 1990. “Safe from Harm” is 27 May 1991, after the album. “Hymn of the Big Wheel,” backed with “Be Thankful for What You’ve Got,” is 10 February 1992, which is a different February and a different volume’s problem. The February that matters here is 1991. The song was a single for almost two months before it was an album track.

That order changes the polite story in which an album is conceived whole and the single is a sample of it. Sometimes that is true. Here the public sequence is the other way. A single in February, an album in April, a further single in May. Shara Nelson and Horace Andy are the singers the album page names. Jonny Dollar is the co-producer with the group. Grantley “Daddy G” Marshall, Robert “3D” Del Naja, Adrian “Tricky” Thaws, and Andrew “Mushroom” Vowles are the members named in the recording credit. Tricky’s later solo album is not this single. This single is a Massive Attack release with a February date, on the way to an April album.

Bristol is in the studio list, not as a mood but as a room: Coach House. London is in the list four times. A record people summarise as a Bristol album was also made in London studios, including Abbey Road. The summary is not false. It is incomplete, and the incompleteness favours the legend. The studio line is more interesting than the legend because it shows a debut moving between a home city and the rooms a label can book. Wild Bunch and Virgin on the label line are the same kind of double name Leftism would carry four years later, independent identity beside a major. In February 1991 the single is what the public had. The album title was not yet a fact on the calendar.

Writing about the song as if the album already existed around it is a way of losing the month. The song can be discussed as a single that introduced singers and a group to a chart before the long-form statement. This piece will not invent a chart position it has not copied off a page in front of it. The date is the claim. 11 February 1991 is early enough, and specific enough, to stand in a February contents list beside records from other years that share only the month. The April album is filed in April. The single stays here, where it was released.
