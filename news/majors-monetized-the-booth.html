They Licensed the Remix Back to You

UMG hands ElevenLabs the mash-up keys the same week Suno drops “industry partner” v6, while Universal is still suing somebody.

format
Essay
date
Sep 12, 2026
author
CJ Archer
words
641 words · 3 min
photo
Photo: Jana C., public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

Dance culture invented the remix as a habit. A white label, a dubplate, a kid in a bedroom with two copies of the same 12" and a reason to stay up. The majors spent decades chasing that feeling into clearance forms. This week they sold a cleaner version of it back.

On 10 September, Universal Music Group and ElevenLabs announced a multi-year licensing agreement and a wider strategic collaboration (Santa Monica / New York dateline, the whole press-release geography). First major-label deal for ElevenLabs, Music Week noted. The pitch, from the UMG release itself: a new AI music creation platform, still in development, where fans can co-create via remixes, mashups, new track interpretations, personalized vocal experiences, on licensed music, with artist participation. Opt-in, The Verge reported. Artists choose. The platform sits separate from ElevenLabs’ Music API and ElevenMusic. Lucian Grainge talked about artists, songwriters, and fans at the center; ElevenLabs’ Mati Staniszewski talked fair compensation. You have heard these sentences before. They describe a product.

A day earlier, 9 September, Suno launched v6: flagship model, exploratory v6-wild for Pro/Premier, v6-mini free for everyone, older models headed for retirement. Developed with industry partners including Warner Music Group, BMG, and Believe, per Suno’s own blog. Reuters framed the part the chat already smelled: Suno settled a copyright suit with Warner and signed licensing deals; Udio settled with UMG and Warner last year. Billboard’s FAQ the next day was colder. Suno still faces ongoing litigation from UMG, Sony Music, SOCAN, Koda. Recently lost a German copyright case brought by GEMA. Responsible AI and still-in-court can share a calendar week. That is the real gossip.

Warner’s Robert Kyncl, in an internal memo Billboard cited, called the new model “fully licensed.” Prior unlicensed-trained model to shut down, revenue to partners. Suno CPO Jack Brody told Billboard the revenue share goes to rights holders (Warner, BMG) and is not framed as payment for training. Suno avoids the word “license” in public while still litigating UMG and Sony. Brody told The Verge v6 was “trained from the ground up” on a new set of data that does not include the same data previous models used, including licensed WMG/BMG/Believe content plus “user data.” CEO Mikey Shulman, on the Suno blog and to Reuters, called v6 a blueprint for how AI and the industry strengthen each other. He also talked opt-in experiences built around individual artists who get paid when they participate. Billboard had him saying the v6 training mix is “partner data” plus “user interactions,” and claiming no Universal, Sony, or Kobalt songs in that partner data. User interactions, user data: the trades flag the ambiguity, and we will too.

UMG, meanwhile, is also developing an AI platform with Udio, The Verge noted, and has prior AI licensing deals with Spotify, Nvidia, Klay. Read it as a portfolio. One major sells you a licensed remix sandbox with ElevenLabs. Another major helps train the generator that still sits across a courtroom from the first major. The booth used to resolve that kind of contradiction with a look. Now it resolves with a FAQ.

What got invented downstairs (the mash-up, the unofficial edit, the personalized vocal you weren’t supposed to have) is being reissued upstairs as a feature list. That is not nothing. Opt-in is better than scrape-and-pray. A revenue rail is better than a lawsuit that only lawyers eat. But do not confuse a multi-year licensing agreement with the culture that made remixes matter. We made the remix because the night needed a new version before clearance did. They are selling the remix because the catalog needs a new engagement loop.

Same week, two press cycles. One word they all like: responsible. One word the Billboard FAQ will not let you forget: ongoing. Keep both on the desk. The majors monetized the booth. The floor still knows who built it.
