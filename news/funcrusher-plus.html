Funcrusher Plus

Company Flow released the album on 22 July 1997 on Rawkus. It was recorded at Ozone, No Mystery, in New York. An instrumental volume followed in 1999.

Funcrusher Plus is Company Flow’s debut studio album, released 22 July 1997 on Rawkus. The studio is Ozone, No Mystery, in New York, with recording dated 1994 to 1997. The length is 73 minutes and 52 seconds. The group produced it. A 1995 EP, Funcrusher, precedes the album in the chronology. Singles listed are “8 Steps to Perfection” in 1996 and “Blind” in 1997. In 2009 the album was reissued on Definitive Jux, the label El-P later founded, which by then had already released Cannibal Ox’s The Cold Vein. The reissue is not the July object. The July object is the Rawkus album. Wikipedia calls it a landmark independent hip-hop release, which is the page’s evaluative sentence and can be quoted as such. The dates do not need the adjective. The adjective shows how the page expects the record to be remembered.

The instrumental consequence is in the chronology line: Little Johnny from the Hospitul: Breaks & Instrumentals Vol. 1, 1999. Company Flow did not only make a vocal debut and stop. They issued a volume that names itself as breaks and instrumentals. That later title is the group’s own description of a practice: the breaks were separable, and worth a volume number. The Cold Vein’s instrumental companion in 2002, under El-P’s presenting credit, is a second instance of the same habit from the same producer’s circle. July 1997 is the vocal album. 1999 is the instrumental volume. The order is the lesson. Instrumental hip-hop, in this catalogue, is not the original and the rap a distraction. The rap album is the debut. The breaks volume is the follow-up that says the beats can stand.

Rawkus in 1997 is a different independent from Definitive Jux in 2001. The reissue on the later label folds the debut back into El-P’s subsequent home, which is a catalogue decision a listener should see rather than treat as the original home. Original homes matter. Rawkus is where the debut was released. Seventy-four minutes is a long independent debut, in the same range as The Cold Vein and as several of the jungle albums in this run. Length, across those records, is the shared ambition: use the CD. Company Flow used it on a New York indie in the same summer the Prodigy and Roni Size were using major-distributed labels in Britain for very different music. Same season, different coasts, no shared scene required. The month is July because 22 July is the date. The rest is coincidence of the calendar, which is the honest kind.

El-P’s name is the bridge a reader will use, and it should be used with the credits in order. He is part of Company Flow on this album. He is the producer and label head on The Cold Vein four years later. He is the presenter of the instrumental Cannibal Ox record. Three roles, three objects, three dates. July’s page is only the first role. Stretching it into a career essay would steal from the May volume and from the 1999 instrumental title, which deserves its own sentence and has one. The debut can be allowed to be a debut: Rawkus, New York, 22 July 1997, produced by the group, followed by a breaks volume the group titled as such.
