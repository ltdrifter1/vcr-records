Funcrusher Plus

Company Flow’s album, released by Rawkus on 22 July 1997, is independent hip-hop at full density: El-P’s production, two rappers, and no interest in the radio’s manners.

Rawkus, and a full length

Funcrusher Plus is the debut album by Company Flow, released by Rawkus on 22 July 1997. El-P, Bigg Jus, and Mr. Len recorded it between 1994 and 1997 at Ozone and at No Mystery in New York. It runs 73 minutes and 52 seconds. The group produced it. An EP called Funcrusher had come out in 1995 and given the album its name and its reputation among people who traded tapes. The singles from the album chapter are 8 Steps to Perfection in 1996 and Blind in 1997. Little Johnny from the Hospitul, an instrumental record, followed in 1999. A Definitive Jux reissue came later still. The 1997 Rawkus album is the object. It is the one that made independent hip-hop sound like a decision rather than like a budget.

The sound is claustrophobic on purpose. El-P’s beats are dense, noisy, and rhythmic, full of scrap, siren, and drum patterns that knock and also scrape. There is very little of the warm soul loop that was the other New York style in those years. Company Flow were not trying to be that. They were trying to be a wall you could still rap on. Bigg Jus and El-P trade verses that are packed, referential, hostile to a casual listen, and often funny once the hostility lets you in. Mr. Len’s cuts are part of the arrangement, not a guest spot at the end of a bar. The mix is dry and loud. A hook, when it exists, is a chant or a phrase repeated until it becomes architecture. Blind is the most famous door. 8 Steps to Perfection is the other. The album around them is longer and stranger, and the length is the point. Seventy-four minutes of this density is a worldview.

Manners, declined

What the record is against is as audible as what it is for. It is against the shiny guest verse, the sung R&B hook rented for the radio, the skit that exists to prove a crew has friends. The positive case is a crew that can produce, rap, and sequence a CD without asking a major for taste. Rawkus, in 1997, was the label that could carry that case into shops. Company Flow were its sternest early proof. The influence runs forward into Definitive Jux, the label El-P would build, and into The Cold Vein, where his production finds a different pair of voices. Funcrusher Plus is the workshop: the beats already enormous, the attitude already set, the independent release already a full length rather than a complaint about not having one.

The 1995 EP is the sketch. Funcrusher Plus is the finished wall. Hearing them in that order, if you can find the EP, shows the group lengthening an idea rather than sanding it down for the longer format. The album is harsher than a debut is supposed to be, and more complete. Rawkus let it be both. That permission, in July 1997, was the news.

The received status is a landmark, and the status is earned by the listening, not by the plaque. You can hear why a generation of rappers who felt crowded by the chart used this album as permission. You can also hear why it never became furniture. The mixes do not recede. The jokes do not translate into slogans. Five years from now Blind will still be the track a new listener is handed, and the album will still be the heavier gift. Play it loud. The scrap in the beat is composition. The density of the rhyme is the same instinct. Rawkus, 22 July 1997, Ozone and No Mystery, 73 minutes and 52 seconds, three people who declined the manners of the year and left a record that still feels like pressure.
