New Forms

A double, on purpose

New Forms is the album by Roni Size / Reprazent, released by Talkin’ Loud on 23 June 1997. It runs 139 minutes and 54 seconds. The producers are Roni Size, DJ Die, Krust, and DJ Suv, a Bristol crew working as one group rather than as a compilation. The music is drum and bass with jazz harmony, live bass, and vocals that are allowed to be songs. Britain put it at number 8 and kept it on the albums chart for 46 weeks. In 1997 it won the Mercury Prize. The ceremony’s exact day is a detail the music does not need. The fact is the prize: a drum-and-bass album, long and instrumental in spirit even where it sings, judged against the year’s rock and pop and preferred.

The singles are the doors. Share the Fall came out on 19 May 1997 and reached number 37. Heroes on 11 September reached number 31. Brown Paper Bag on 27 October reached number 20. Watching Windows on 28 April 1998 reached number 28. Share the Fall is the early statement of the method, a vocal piece that still has the break at the center, issued a month before the album so the album would not arrive as a rumor. Brown Paper Bag is the one a jazz listener can enter through, an acoustic bass figure against a drum-and-bass grid, melodic and rhythmically severe at once. Heroes and Watching Windows keep the voices in the story. None of them is a pop concession with the breaks removed. The edit for the chart is still the music.

Jazz chords, jungle tempo

The sound of the double album is space. Two hours is a risk in a genre that had perfected the 12-inch, where a track could be devastating for six minutes and then stop. New Forms uses the length to change rooms. A vocal track hands off to an instrumental. A jazz chord holds while the break sprints. The bass, often played rather than only programmed, gives the low end a human attack. Size’s programming is the grid. Die, Krust, and Suv keep the grid from becoming one man’s habit. You can hear four musical personalities agreeing. The agreement is Bristol’s Full Cycle world translated into an album a major-affiliated label, Talkin’ Loud, could put in a shop. The translation did not sand the tempo. It organised it.

What the record changed is the status of the form. Jungle and drum and bass had already made albums. Gerald Simpson’s Black Secret Technology, Goldie’s Timeless, the compilations that tried to be documents. New Forms is the one that walked into the prize that British music uses when it wants to say an album is the year’s serious object. After it, a long drum-and-bass record could be discussed as composition, with track titles and a group name, not only as a DJ’s fuel. The influence has a double edge. Some later albums copied the politeness and lost the break’s danger. The original still has the danger. The breaks are fast. The bass is heavy. The jazz is not a costume. It is harmonic information.

Five years from now the Mercury line will be the biography, and the music will be the reason to ignore the biography for two hours. Play Brown Paper Bag if you need the thesis in one track. Play Share the Fall if you want the vocal argument. Play the album if you want the scale, which is the actual claim: drum and bass can be as long as a jazz double LP and not run out of ideas. Talkin’ Loud, 23 June 1997, 139 minutes and 54 seconds, four producers, a British number 8, a prize. The newness in the title was real. The forms were breaks, chords, and voices. They still fit.
