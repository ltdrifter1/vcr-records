Jack Your Body

Steve “Silk” Hurley’s single is number one on the Official Chart dated 18 January 1987. The same site’s song history dates that week differently. The record still went to the top.

House records did not arrive in Britain as an idea. They arrived as stock. Steve “Silk” Hurley’s “Jack Your Body,” on DJ International, catalogue number LON117, has a first chart date of 10 January 1987 on the Official Charts song page. That page then lists it at number 2 on 17 January, number 1 on 24 January, and number 1 again on 31 January. Two weeks at the top. Nine weeks on the chart, through 7 March. The label and the catalogue number are printed on the same page.

The chart browser on the same organisation tells the week differently. The page headed “Official Singles Chart on 18/1/1987,” covering 18 January to 24 January 1987, lists “Jack Your Body” at number one, with last week at 2. A tally kept at London Hearts uses 18 January 1987 as the start and 31 January as the end, two weeks, and adds the note that it was the first US house recording to make UK number one. The Guardian, in a 2011 series on dance history, dates the event 18 January 1987 and says the same thing in plainer language: Farley “Jackmaster” Funk’s “Love Can’t Turn Around” had reached number 10 in 1986, and Hurley’s record was the first to hit number one. It arrived, the Guardian wrote, with no promotion, a video cut from old black-and-white films, and barely any lyrics.

The disagreement is about which day the week is named for. It is not a disagreement about the fact that the record sat at the top for two weeks in the second half of January 1987. A magazine that cares about metadata should print the conflict instead of sanding it. Sunday-start and Saturday-end are both ways of pointing at one chart week. The song page and the chart page are both Official Charts. They can be quoted side by side. What cannot be done, honestly, is to pick the prettier date and drop the other.

The Guardian’s description is the musical criticism the paperwork supports. Barely any lyrics. No promotion that the paper of record of that later article could find worth naming. A video assembled from existing film. The record did not persuade the country with a personality. It persuaded a chart with a pattern. Chicago house, by January 1987, was already a local history in the city that made it. In Britain that month it became a number. Numbers are crude. They are also public. “Jack Your Body” is the week the crude number and the local history met, and the archives still do not share a single calendar date for the meeting. The meeting happened anyway.

Sources

  • Official Charts, song history: DJ International LON117, first chart date 10 January 1987, two weeks at number one.
  • Official Charts, singles chart dated 18 January 1987: “Jack Your Body” at number one.
  • The Guardian, 15 June 2011: Alexis Petridis’s dance-history note on the 18 January 1987 chart.
  • London Hearts number-one log: 18 January to 31 January 1987, two weeks.
