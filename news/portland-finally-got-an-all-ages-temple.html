Portland Finally Got an All-Ages Temple

The Dancin' Bare is a Friends of Noise room now: no liquor, four hundred caps, and the bloodline showed up with a drum kit.

Portland spent more than ten years without a dedicated all-ages room and talked about the gap as if the sky had arranged it. The kids went to basements. The basements got busted. The legal rooms wanted a drink in your hand. A city that only lets you hear music with a drink in your hand is a bar city with better bands than it deserves.

February 2026, the former Dancin' Bare reopened as The Off Beat: Friends of Noise, four hundred capacity, all ages, 8440 N Interstate. Willamette Week walked the room on 16 August 2026 and called it what the rest of us had been waiting to call it: Portland's only all-ages music venue, the first dedicated all-ages room in this city in more than a decade. File that under overdue, not under miracle.

The Bare had a long first life, 1989 to 2020. Buildings outlast uses, and this is the good version of that sentence. A room that used to sell a different night now sells a floor a sixteen-year-old can stand on without a fake ID and without a security guy performing adulthood at them. No alcohol is the whole arrangement. You cannot run an all-ages room and a liquor program and then act surprised when the kids get shown the door.

Friends of Noise has been doing the unglamorous version of this since 2016. Executive director Andre Middleton. Money that actually showed up: ARPA, the City of Portland, Meyer Memorial Trust. Read that list twice if you have ever tried to keep a DIY calendar alive on door take and a group chat. Public money in an all-ages room is how you stop asking a high-school band to subsidize a landlord. The city that spent a decade without a dedicated room finally wrote a cheque toward one. Night Shift will take the cheque and still side-eye the decade.

You want the bloodline. This is Portland: we keep score by who taught whom to be loud.

Umbraphile, out of Grant High School, with Lucian Landey on drums. His mom is Jody Bleyle: Hazel, Team Dresch, the Kill Rock Stars nervous system, the riot grrrl register this city is always pretending it invented last week. The kid behind the kit is why an all-ages room has to exist: so the next Bleyle does not have to wait until they can hold a pint to learn what a stage feels like.

Team Dresch did not come out of a 21+ marketing plan. Hazel did not come out of a destination-nightlife deck. They came out of rooms that let underage bodies be in the same air as the amp. Portland forgot that for a decade and then got to reopen a shell on Interstate and call it a return. Fine. Take the return. Do not rewrite the gap as inevitable.

No liquor means the night has a different clock: you are not there to close a tab. Four hundred cap is big enough to feel like a hall and small enough that a local bill does not drown. The Off Beat is that room with the bar turned off and the age policy turned on.

House kids, jungle kids, beatmakers who still think a sampler is a personality: this is your building too, even if the early calendar reads more punk than peak-time. All-ages is a structure, not a genre. The structure is what Portland, Vancouver, and Seattle have been short of. Vancouver is inspecting the unlicensed version. Seattle just lost two mid-size rooms and sold the basement to comedy. Portland, for once, did the opposite of a shutdown: it opened a legal floor the kids can enter.

Willamette Week's August walk-through is the public record. A 2016 nonprofit finally got a dedicated hall. A 1989 to 2020 building got a second life that does not require a drink in your hand. City money, federal recovery money, a trust. No alcohol, four hundred bodies, North Interstate.

If you are twenty-seven and you think all-ages is someone else's problem, sit down. The rooms you want at 2 a.m. are downstream of the rooms that exist at 8 p.m. Scenes that only start at liquor age become scenes that only know how to sell a drink. The beat-tape kid, the jungle kid with a USB and a coat, the house DJ who learned to mix in a basement because there was no hall: they needed this building in 2016, and they are getting it in 2026. Late is still better than never, and never was the working plan.

Jody Bleyle's kid on drums in a Friends of Noise room is continuity a tourism paragraph cannot fake. Somebody's mom made the '90s loud. Somebody's kid is making a Tuesday loud on Interstate, in a room that will not card them out.

The Off Beat is at 8440 N Interstate: four hundred capacity, no alcohol, Friends of Noise. Portland spent a decade without this room. In February 2026 the building reopened.
