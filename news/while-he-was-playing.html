While He Was Playing

On Thursday, October 8, at PRST on Praterstraße, somebody took Demuja's USB out of the CDJ mid-set. The music stopped because the stick had left the machine. He has a spare and a backup at home. The unreleased tracks left with someone who had been dancing.

posted by cj archer · Oct 12, 2026

Last Thursday the song stopped for a reason you could hold in one hand. Bernhard Weiss, the Salzburg-born producer who works as Demuja, was mid-set at PRST, Praterstraße 18, in Vienna's second district, when someone took his USB stick out of the CDJ. He posted it afterward. FAZEmag, quoting the Instagram, has him writing that last Thursday someone stole the stick out of the CDJ while he was playing. Loop Rituals, filing the same post on Monday, carried the detail the people inside already had: for a while, it went quiet.

PRST is a small club with a particular building behind it. Wien.info describes the address as a former television station, the dancefloor set in the old recording studio, a room built inside a room so the neighbours never have to learn the kick. Demuja has stood there before. On March 24, 2023, the club billed a night under his name. On May 8, 2024, he invited Ryan Elliott to the same street number. Thursday was a player in a studio that used to record, on a block where his name has already been on the flyer.

What left in that hand was a collection he says took years, and the money those years cost, plus music he had not put out. A follow-up, as Loop Rituals reported it, put the hand in the crowd. Whoever took the stick had been standing with everyone else, close enough to reach the player, which on a dancefloor this size is not a long reach. Spare sticks ride in the gig bag, and a backup sits at home. He priced the plastic at fifty euros and set that number aside. Loop Rituals has him hoping karma finds the person. What bothered him was the behaviour. He also wrote, and both German desks ran it, that he was staying with the 99 percent who already understand a club runs on people who leave the thing making the night where it is. The community, in his wording, is strong.

An extension of the person who built it

Honey Dijon answered in the comments. FAZEmag's German rendering of that comment calls the loss traumatic, and calls the collection an extension of the person who built it. Under the same post, Loop Rituals logged Patrick Pulsinger, Radio Slave and Move D. FAZEmag also had Iris Menza, Chloé Caillet and And Hazel, who wrote that it was disappointing to find people in the scene with so little respect for the work. Nobody in that thread was mourning a memory stick. They were mourning a library compiled in public, one record at a time, then removed in a single reach.

The same gear for fifteen years

Twelve days earlier he had been on GROOVE's podcast, talking like a man who still trusts a small rig. GROOVE Podcast 519 went up on September 30. He recorded the mix on two XDJ-700s and an old DJM-250, “the same gear I have had for 15 years now.” Asked what he had in mind, he said he wanted the dubbier music he is making at the moment, lifted enough for a night, and that he tries not to overthink a mix. “Not prepare too much and just go with the flow, see where it takes me.” Four of the twelve tracks in the published list are credited to him and titled, simply, Unreleased.

That working method was already on the page before anybody reached for the port. On September 9 his Bandcamp put out Demuja presents Dubmuja, a live-jam album he described to GROOVE as finally out: fifteen tracks, dub as the brief. Loop Rituals reports that he has put the number of finished tracks on the hard drive at about 400, a lot of them dripped onto that Bandcamp, beside the records that went to Permanent Vacation, Chiwax, Peach Discs and his own imprint, MUJA, which he started in 2017. The hard drive with the 400 is the copy at home. The stick that left on Thursday is a different object, and he has not published a count for it. What he has said is that unreleased music was on it, and that the copy at home does not settle the taking.

The longer biography matters here because it explains the pile. In an April 2020 interview with Austrian Music Export he told Christoph Benkeser that his father was a drummer, that he came up in percussion ensembles, and that a breakdance crew he was in won the Austrian championship twice before house got him at an afterparty. MUJA, he said then, began because he was tired of sending demos and waiting weeks for a no, and because labels kept asking for one genre and a lid on the rest. At home the studio day started at nine, a deal with the neighbours. He also said that when he is making the music, the dancefloor is out of his head. On Thursday the people dancing reached over and took the crate.

Closer to the song

Over the last year and a half, the stick has been walking toward the speaker.

On June 20, 2025, DJ Hell was at dinner in Milan when his bag went. Mixmag listed the contents from his post: passport, credit cards, cash, car keys, and a USB stick holding music and what he called important data. He had a gig that night at Vacuum, with Jade Removille and Peppe Amore, for NR Magazine. The gig was cancelled. “This is a personal and artistic loss,” he wrote. That stick was still in a restaurant. It never reached a player.

Earlier the same June, Mixmag reported, the Menendez Brothers had a DJ bag taken at fabric in London. They posted video of an attendee grabbing it minutes after the set began. Inside, they said, were USBs of years of collected music and, the clause they underlined, new unreleased tracks they were in the middle of making. The bag was beside the decks. The set had started, and the song kept going.

On October 10, 2025, almost a year before Demuja's Thursday, Loco Dice played Fabrique in Milan. A hard drive and a fully loaded USB were taken from his tour manager's bag in the booth. DJ Mag quoted the post. “The stolen hard drive contains my entire music collection.” He apologized to the artists who had trusted him with unreleased music, and asked anyone who heard a demo they knew from his sets, played by somebody else, to tell his team. The club was checking the booth cameras. “You still have time to come forward anonymously and to make this right, please return what's not yours.” In the same piece DJ Mag remembered that Annie Mac had lost a stick during a gig and later posted a handwritten note from the person who brought it back. Some of these end with a return. Dice's note, when the magazine ran it, was still a request.

Thursday in Vienna is a different photograph. Hell's stick was in a bag at a table. The Brothers' bag left the booth on camera, minutes into the set. Dice's drive was behind him, in someone else's luggage. Demuja's stick was in the CDJ, under the song, doing the job. When that one goes, the bar stops. Everyone who had been dancing heard the theft at the same time, which is more than you can say for a bag.

A room built inside a room will keep a kick off Praterstraße. It will not keep a hand off a port.

Fifty euros, and the other copy

He has been careful, in the follow-ups, to refuse the consolation people reach for first. The spare is already in the bag, the backup is already at home, and fifty euros buys another stick at a shop on the way to the station. A person who only wanted the plastic had a bad night's work. A person who wanted the unreleased has Demuja's unfinished music, and whatever else was riding on that stick, in a pocket, with a silenced bar as proof it was worth taking in the middle.

The backup plays the next gig. It has nothing to say about last Thursday. Last Thursday is a bar that stopped, a hand going back into a crowd, and a producer who had told a magazine, twelve days prior, that four of the tracks in his mix did not have titles yet. The unreleased ones were already how he works. A stranger now has a selection of them, whichever files happened to be loaded for PRST, and the people who can swear to the minute they left are the ones who heard the song go.

He asked to be counted with the 99 percent. Praterstraße 18 still has that majority. It is also missing a stick that cost less than a drink, loaded for a Thursday, gone out the door with someone who had been standing close enough to reach the player.
