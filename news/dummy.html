Dummy

Beth Gibbons. Rain on the window. The other door besides the crate shop.

ListeningOct 14, 2024

posted by cj archer

If you only take one door from this desk, people still hand you Endtroducing. Shadow in the shop. Labor as love. That’s a real record. It is also a certain kind of listening — boys with flashlights, the hunt as personality. Here’s the other door. Portishead, Dummy, 1994. Not a crate flex. A weather.

You know the temperature even if you pretend you don’t. Crackle like a radiator. Bass that lives under the floorboards. A voice that has already decided not to explain itself. This is not peak time. This is the walk home in a coat that isn’t quite dry. This is one lamp and a window doing most of the talking. Beth doesn’t raise herself to be believed. She lets the room come to her.

I put it on in Cumberland when the rain commits, which is most days. “Sour Times” once without performing it for anyone. Then leave the album running. If you start watching the glass instead of your phone, that’s the review. Half of what we put out two years later is still trying to get back to this humidity — the after, the latch, the private mix. Dummy is the original of that weather. The crate shop can wait.
