Dubstep Warz

On 10 January 2006 Mary Anne Hobbs broadcast Dubstep Warz on BBC Radio 1, two hours. The BBC's list of that show, published with the 6 September 2010 repeat, marks most of the entries white. Skream's Midnight Request Line is one of the lines that already says Tempa.

On 10 January 2006 Mary Anne Hobbs broadcast Dubstep Warz on BBC Radio 1. The show ran two hours. BBC Radio 6 Music, introducing a twentieth-anniversary edition, states the original date in those words, and describes the people in it as a very young South London scene: Digital Mystikz, Skream, Kode9 and the Spaceape, Vex'd, Hatcha and Crazy D, Loefah and Sgt. Pokes, and Distance. Complex, marking seven years on 10 January 2013, called it a special edition of her Breezeblock show, and noted that the music was already there before the broadcast. The birth, in that piece, is not the claim. The broadcast is.

The tracklist the BBC itself prints is on the page for the repeat. On 6 September 2010, in what that page calls her last week on Radio 1, the station played the 2006 edition again and called it influential. The music-played list opens with D1, "Untitled", marked white. White, on that list, is the word that sits where other lines say Tempa or Hyperdub. It is the BBC's mark for a record that had not been given a label name in the listing.

Six whites

The next section is headed Mala (Digital Mystikz). Six entries, each marked white. "10 Dread Commandments VIP". "Haunted". "Left Leg Out". A Mala remix of Skream's "Request Line". "All Of A Sudden". "Anti-War Dub". VIP, in the dub tradition the title is borrowing from, is a version: a cut made for the system, not the ordinary shop copy. The list gives the version no label. The low end on the Digital Mystikz records is the thing the section is for. It does not behave like a riff waiting for a chorus. It sits under the bar as weight, and the drums leave it alone. "Anti-War Dub" puts the position in the title. On the 2010 list it is still white.

The line that already had a label

Skream's section is where a label name appears. "Midnight Request Line" is marked Tempa. So are "Tapped", "Glamma", "Deep Concentration", "Rottan", and his remix of Digital Mystikz' "Ancient Memories". "Almighty Father", the Skream remix of Sunship and Warrior Queen, is marked white. "Music 2 Make Us Stagga" is marked white. One section holds both conditions.

"Midnight Request Line" is the track whose title is a telephone and whose line on the list already has a catalogue home. The bass does not hold a single pitch. It swells and thins inside the bar, a modulation rather than a note, and a short figure up above it repeats like a signal. The kick waits. The Mala remix of "Request Line", in the section before, is marked white. The same title is on the list twice: once under Mala, marked white, once as the Tempa issue under Skream. Both were on the same show.

A voice, then a number

The Kode9 and Spaceape section is the one with a voice credited as half of the act. "9 Samurai", "Backward" and "Kingston Dub" are billed to the duo. The Spaceape's voice is spoken, close, and dry. It sits in the foreground and speaks. "Kingston Dub" is the line the 2010 page marks Hyperdub. The track that follows is a different credit: Burial and the Spaceape, "Space Ape", marked white. Untrue, Burial's album on Hyperdub, is dated 5 November 2007 on this desk. The credit here is one white-label track with the Spaceape on it. Kode9 is not on that line.

On the seventh anniversary Hobbs posted the show again. Complex quoted her. "This radio show was considered the global Tipping Point for the sound.. a show that changed history." The same article says the birth of the music precedes the broadcast by a few years, or more, depending on where the flag is planted. A tipping point is a description of an audience arriving. It is not a description of a first record. The list is more specific than the phrase. Most of what the page records had not taken a label name. Tempa and Hyperdub are the exceptions it prints.

The blanks

Hatcha's section, on the 2010 page, leaves eight entries as "Untitled", credited to Benga or to Coki, and marks them white. In that stretch the page does print the Skream remix of "Ancient Memories", and marks it Tempa. Further down, under Hatcha again, a "Request Line" remix is marked white. The page names a person, and a label when it has one, and it prints Untitled when it does not.

Vex'd are five titles, all white: "Saturn", "3rd Choice", a "Wavescape" remix, "Killing Floor", a "Knowledge" remix. Loefah, billed with Sgt. Pokes, has four, all white: "System", "Mud", "Ruffage", "Sukkah". "Mud" sounds like the title. The low end moves as one mass. Distance is split on the page. "My Demons", "Tuning" and "Fallen" appear, then Loefah, then another Hatcha line, then Distance returns with "Cyclops", "Cella", "Night Vision" and "Traffic", each white. The page is not one continuous running order. It is a set of sections, and inside them a mark.

The 2010 list ends on Pinch, "Qawwali", white. The title names a devotional form from somewhere else. It is the last word the repeat's playlist gives, and it is given the same mark as D1 at the top. On 12 January 2026 BBC Radio 6 Music put out a twentieth-anniversary edition and restated the original date: Radio 1, 10 January 2006. The date has not moved. What the 2010 list still explains is the condition of the records that night. A two-hour show on a national station played a South London scene while most of the entries were white. The shops had Tempa and Hyperdub on a handful of lines. The rest of the show did not wait for them.
