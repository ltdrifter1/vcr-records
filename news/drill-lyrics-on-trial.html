Bars On Trial

The Met says drill lyrics prove crimes. Not everyone agrees.

CJ Archer

London’s Met is still putting drill lyrics in front of juries. Art Not Evidence wants the law changed. A report counted lyrics used against 252 defendants in three years. That is a pattern, not a one-off.

The police line is: if someone puts a real crime in a song, it’s fair game. Status, they say. Young people tell on themselves for clout. A verse is still not a confession. A metaphor is not a map. The booth is not a witness box.

You can hate a song and still refuse to let the state treat a bar like CCTV. If the case needs the music to stand up, the case was already thin.
