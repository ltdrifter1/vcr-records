Born I Sat Down

A DC producer on tour with Plum Village monks. Bars over singing bowls. The scene treats stillness like a failure. He’s not buying it.

CJ Archer

Underground hip-hop runs on hustle. Grind, drop, promote, repeat, and don’t stop until the catalog says otherwise. So it’s worth noticing when somebody steps out of that loop — not to burn out quieter, but to sit down.

DC producer and MC Born I spent last spring on tour with the monks and nuns of Plum Village, the monastery founded by Thich Nhat Hanh, doing what he calls a Hip Hop Soundbath. Bars over singing bowls instead of drums. Built for a room that’s meant to breathe, not move. His most recent album, AMIDA, treats a verse the way a breath practice treats an exhale: you don’t rush it, because rushing it defeats the point.

He comes up through the same lineage most of the scene claims — Wu-Tang, Andre 3000, Alice Coltrane — but he’s spent as much time as a certified meditation teacher as he has behind a mic. His teacher told him, at some point, to drop the idea of being a “good” practitioner altogether. Performing the identity of someone who has it figured out is its own kind of noise. His label is named after the same lesson: You Are Enough. Not once you’ve blown up. Now.

It’s a necessary correction for a scene that treats stillness as a failure state — dead air on the timeline, a gap in the release calendar, proof you’ve stalled. Rooms are getting quieter on purpose lately: breathwork before the headliner, sound baths opening for the rave, DJs building fifteen minutes of near-silence into a peak-time set just to see who stays. None of it is soft. Sitting in a room with nothing to hide behind takes more nerve than dropping a bass at the right moment.

Every record we’ve put out here takes longer than it should by industry math, and we’ve never had a good answer for why beyond “it wasn’t ready.” Maybe that’s the same thing Born I is teaching a room of strangers with a singing bowl. The track doesn’t need saving. Neither do you. Both just need the time to actually arrive.
