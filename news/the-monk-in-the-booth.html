Born I Sat Down

DC’s Born I tours with Plum Village monks — bars over singing bowls, and a scene that still treats stillness like failure.

posted by cj archer · Apr 2, 2024

The underground treats stillness like a failure state: dead air on the timeline, a gap in the release calendar, proof you’ve stalled. So notice when somebody steps out of the grind loop — not to burn out quieter, but to sit down on purpose.

DC producer and MC Born I spent a spring on tour with the monks and nuns of Plum Village, Thich Nhat Hanh’s lineage, doing Hip Hop Soundbaths: bars over singing bowls instead of drums. Built for a room that’s meant to breathe, not perform peak-time. His album AMIDA treats a verse like an exhale — you don’t rush it, because rushing defeats the point.

Same lineage everybody claims (Wu-Tang, André 3000, Alice Coltrane), plus years as a meditation teacher. His teacher told him to drop being a “good” practitioner. Performing enlightenment is just another kind of noise. The label name says the quiet part: You Are Enough. Not after the blow-up. Now.

Rooms are getting quieter on purpose — breathwork before the headliner, sound baths opening the rave, DJs slipping near-silence into a set to see who stays. None of it is soft. Sitting with nothing to hide behind takes more nerve than landing the drop.

Every record we’ve sat on takes longer than industry math allows. Maybe that’s the same lesson as a singing bowl in a room of strangers: the track doesn’t need saving. Neither do you. Both need time to arrive.
