The Kitchen Was the Club

In April 2021 there was no door and no cloakroom, just a link, a stick of incense, and socks on linoleum while someone DJed from another province.

format
Essay
date
Apr 12, 2021
author
CJ Archer
words
544 words · 2 min
photo
Photo: Gogerr

Retired on 23 October 2026. The piece was a lockdown mood sketch with no named night, no named room, and nothing a reader could check. The text below stays so this address still resolves. Do not cite it.

April 2021 and the ritual starts before the speaker. Somebody lights something because the air needs a job. Somebody pastes a Zoom into the chat like it’s a pin dropped for people who can’t leave the house. Somebody is DJing from a bedroom in another province and you are in socks on the linoleum like that’s a floor that can hold a night, which somehow it can, which somehow it has to. The rooms are still a brochure: photographs of doors you can’t walk through, capacity fantasies, posters for months that keep sliding into the next unfortunately. The kitchen is the club: kettle on, stick lit, link pasted, mute yourself unless you’re the one with the USB, and even then keep your voice low enough that the neighbours don’t learn the drop and call a different kind of door policy.

In 1999 this would have been a mixtape on the counter and someone doing their eyes in the toaster while the night argued with itself in the hallway, somebody’s dad asleep, somebody’s shoes already in a pile. In 2021 the mixtape is a Discord booth and the toaster still works, same warped reflection, same small vanity that says you intended to leave even when leaving isn’t legal, even when the only curb is the one outside a building you’re not supposed to gather in. You do not clap on the two. You do not talk over the drop. You send a heart in the chat and you mean it, which is a strange new kind of tenderness: applause as a pixel, presence as a green dot, friendship as a grid of faces trying not to cry when a familiar song makes the apartment feel briefly infinite. The afters are the same apartment because there is nowhere else. That’s not a punchline. That’s the liturgy we built when the city closed its hands.

When they open the real door later this year, some of you will pretend this never counted, that the night only starts when a bouncer looks at your ID and a cloakroom takes your coat for money and a stranger stamps your wrist like proof. It counted. The body learned the night without a PA, learned how to make weather with a stick and a playlist and the discipline of mute. If you still put incense on before the aux, that’s not nostalgia. That’s the practice you built when the only room you could trust was yours, and the only door policy was whether you answered the link, and the only cloakroom was a chair that held a coat for a street that wasn’t open yet. Keep the practice. Keep the mute when the drop asks for it. Keep the heart in the chat for the nights when a pixel is all the applause a bedroom DJ gets. The kitchen was the club. Some of us still know how to make it one.

CJ Archer
