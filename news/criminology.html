Criminology

Raekwon released the single on 26 June 1995. Only Built 4 Cuban Linx... followed on 1 August, produced by RZA, recorded in his basement studio in New York.

Only Built 4 Cuban Linx... is Raekwon’s debut album, released 1 August 1995 by Wu-Tang Records, Loud, and RCA. RZA produced it. The studio credit is his basement in New York. The album debuted at number 4 on the Billboard 200 and number 2 on the Top R&B/Hip-Hop Albums chart, with 130,000 copies in the first week. It was certified gold on 2 October 1995 and platinum on 24 February 2020. The UK albums peak is 85. The August date is the album’s. This volume is June, because “Criminology” is dated 26 June 1995. “Heaven & Hell” is 24 October 1994. “Ice Cream” is listed only as 1995, with no day, so it is not given a month here.

A June single and an August album is the same pattern as “Shook Ones, Part II” and The Infamous, and as “Share the Fall” and New Forms. The pattern is how rap and dance albums were sold in the middle of the 1990s: a song crosses the public threshold, then the sequence follows. RZA’s basement is the production fact that matters to a page about instrumental practice. The debut of a Wu-Tang rapper was made by the group’s producer in a basement, not at the New York rooms — Battery, Unique, D&D — named on Illmatic and The Infamous. A basement studio is a specific claim. It is not “lo-fi” as a compliment. It is a room in a credit. The album that came out of it opened at number 4 in the United States. The room and the chart can be printed in the same paragraph without one cancelling the other.

Ghostface Killah’s role on the album is described on the page as part of a loose narrative, Raekwon as Lex Diamonds and Ghostface as Tony Starks, with most of the Clan appearing and with Cappadonna, Blue Raspberry, and Nas as further names. Nas’s appearance is called the first collaboration with a non-Wu-Tang affiliate. That is a discography fact about a border. June’s single sits before all of that narrative was available as an album. Listeners who bought “Criminology” on 26 June did not yet have the August sequence. Writing as if they did is how album reviews eat their own singles. The single can be about the producer and the date. The narrative can wait for a reader who puts the album on.

Loud appears again. The Infamous, in April, was a Loud album. Cuban Linx, in August, is a Loud album with Wu-Tang and RCA beside the name. The label is a corridor between Queens and Staten Island records in the same year, which is a plainer map than a myth of one New York sound. June holds the single that walked the corridor first. The basement, the first-week number, and the platinum certificate twenty-five years later belong to the album, and they are cited here so the single is not left without its consequence. The consequence has its own month. This page only has to point at it.
