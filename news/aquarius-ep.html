Five Hundred Copies

Boards of Canada’s Aquarius EP is listed as 5 January 1998 on Skam, catalogue KMAS001: a 7-inch, 500 copies, before the album that made the name.

The album people mean, when they say the name, is Music Has the Right to Children, and that album is dated 20 April 1998. The record that belongs to January is smaller, and the smallness is the point. On the Wikipedia discography, the EP Aquarius is listed as released 5 January 1998, on Skam, catalogue KMAS001. The format line reads: 500 units, limited 7-inch, 33⅓ RPM. In the column that discography labels as the UK physical chart, the EP is given a peak of 2. This piece will not inflate a physical-chart column into the main albums chart. The table is already specific. Five hundred copies. A 7-inch. A January date. An album still three and a half months away.

Boards of Canada are described, on the same discography page, as a Scottish electronic duo. Their earlier EP Twoism is dated August 1995 on their own Music70 label, later reissued by Warp. Hi Scores is December 1996, on Skam. Aquarius is the third of those short records, and the one that falls in the first week of a year. The debut album that follows, on Warp, Skam, and Matador, was made at Hexagon Sun in the Pentland Hills, with recording dated 1995 to 1997. The January EP does not carry that studio line in the table. What it carries is a count of objects. Five hundred is not a metaphor for rarity. It is a pressing figure printed in a discography. Either the figure is right or the table needs a correction. Until then, the figure is what the public page says.

A limited 7-inch at 33⅓ is a slow, short format. It is not the CD album that charted, barely, at 193 in the UK when April came. The path from one to the other is the story the dates tell without any help from a myth about anonymity. August 1995, a self-released EP. December 1996, Skam. January 1998, five hundred singles. April 1998, the album on three labels. Warp is not in the January line. Skam is. The independent label that had already issued Hi Scores issued the 7-inch that opened the year, and the larger label joined for the album. That order is ordinary and worth keeping ordinary. Scene history likes to start at the famous object. The famous object, here, has a catalogue number sitting in front of it with a quantity attached.

Scottish, in the discography’s own wording, is the place-word the page is willing to use. The Pentland Hills belong to the album entry, not automatically to the EP. A review that drags a studio into a single it has not documented is decorating. The EP can be written about as a count, a speed, a label, and a date. Those four facts are enough to put it in a January volume. The album can wait for April, where its own date puts it. Night Shift’s job is not to make the small record famous by association. It is to leave the association in the right order.

Sources

  • Wikipedia, Boards of Canada discography: Aquarius, 5 January 1998, Skam KMAS001, 500 copies; album date 20 April 1998.
  • Wikipedia, “Music Has the Right to Children”: Hexagon Sun, Pentland Hills, Warp / Skam / Matador, 20 April 1998.
