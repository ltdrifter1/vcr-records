Oil and Chrome

SOPHIE died in Athens. The oil didn’t dry.

posted by cj archer · Jan 31, 2021

January 30, Athens. SOPHIE died and the timeline did what timelines do — a photograph, a statement, a bunch of boys who had used “hyperpop” like a sticker. The kids who actually wanted to become a sound already knew. The oil wasn’t a metaphor. The chrome wasn’t a costume. She made records that felt like a body deciding to be plastic on purpose, then deciding that plastic was holy.

OIL OF EVERY PEARL’S UN-INSIDES was 2018. “BIPP” was older. By 2021 the copycats had a festival slot and she still sounded like the only person in the building. That’s the tell. You can steal the metallic snare. You cannot steal the joke and the wound arriving in the same bar. Everybody who ever put on a latex top and called it a political act was borrowing her weather.

The rooms were closed. That made it worse and also cleaner. You heard the news in a kitchen. You put on “It’s Okay to Cry” or “Immaterial” and you did not pretend this was a thinkpiece about the future of pop. It was SOPHIE from Glasgow who moved the floor without asking if the floor was ready. PC Music got a paragraph. Charli got a paragraph. The paragraph that mattered was the group chat that just said fuck and then a voice note of somebody crying into a cheap speaker.

Do not make her a saint. She did not ask. Play the records. Leave the oil on. The future she already finished is still wet. If your night still sounds like metal trying to become skin, that’s not a trend. That’s the work, unfinished because it has to be.
