Five Records from Cumberland

Five L.T. Drifta titles on Club Copy, read against Bandcamp where the page and the sleeve disagree.

A catalogue is a claim about order. L.T. Drifta’s Club Copy claim, printed on the artist page, is seven titles: If Ya Want (CC021), Nia Archives - Get Me Down (Molly's Hazy Jungle Edit) (CC020), Champion Sound (CC001), Classic Jazz (CC003), Summer Madness (CC006), Lakeshore West 23:55 (CC008), and Three 4 Zone (CC014). The page also marks Classic Jazz, Summer Madness, and Lakeshore West 23:55 with a second code — VCR026D1, VCR026D2, VCR026D3 — and does not say what the code means. This piece will not invent a meaning. It reads the seven objects a buyer can actually open, and where clubcopy.ca and Bandcamp disagree it follows Bandcamp. That is the sleeve outside the label’s own site.

The artist page tags the music jungle: “the soulful side of jungle and hip-hop.” The roster says “Jungle / Breaks” and “the soulful side of the electronic underground.” None of the seven release pages uses jungle as its genre line. Champion Sound is filed as instrumental hip-hop, Classic Jazz as jazz, and the other five as electronic. The word on the artist page is a direction of ear. The word on the release page is the object.

Champion Sound is the first number and, on the public calendar, not the first arrival. The Club Copy page prints 4 October 2025. Bandcamp dates it 15 December 2025, and the label’s catalogue data agrees. One track, 4:53, $1.50 CAD. The page copy is “The room after the bridge. Crate heat. Sessions that don’t apologize for staying quiet.” A crate, in hip-hop and in the break music that fed jungle, is the pile of older records a producer answers. The page names no sample. The opening number is a single instrumental, described as a session rather than a peak. The title has named other famous records. The copy walks that back to a quiet room. The number says origin. The date says two longer records were already public.

Classic Jazz is the title that reached Bandcamp first, on 4 October 2025. CC003, also marked VCR026D1, digital $9 CAD, genre line jazz. The page’s sentence is “Seven standards, rebuilt. White label.” “Rebuilt” is the strongest verb the label uses about these records, and it is not explained. Rebuilt could mean a sample, a replay, or a new piece wearing an old name. The sleeve does not choose. The contents have to be taken from Bandcamp, because the Club Copy page is a different contents page. Bandcamp’s order and timings are “Naima” (4:39), “My One and Only Love” (5:34), “Sentimental Mood” (5:12), “Four” (3:51), “Colors of You” (4:08), “Variations In Fall” (5:04), and “Taste Honey” (4:14). The sum is 32:42. The site prints a total of 27:30, times “Naima” at 7:54, titles the second track “One and Only Love,” and swaps the last two, with “Taste of Honey” before “Variations In Fall.” Until the site is corrected, the public album is the Bandcamp one.

The titles are a reading list, which is not a sample credit. “Naima” is John Coltrane’s. “My One and Only Love” is a standard. “Sentimental Mood” sits one word away from Ellington’s “In a Sentimental Mood,” and the page never says they are the same piece. “Four” is the Miles Davis composition. “Taste Honey” is close to “A Taste of Honey,” and spelled differently. None of that proves what is in the audio. It shows which names were put on a jazz-filed album the same season as an electronic album reaching for the same shelf. Seven tracks, the longest 5:34, is a sequence kept short. “White label,” beside a $9 album that already has a cover, is the other claim: a test-pressing word on a dressed object. Both phrases are the label’s.

Summer Madness, CC006, VCR026D2, followed on 14 November 2025. Eight tracks, electronic, $9. Bandcamp times them “Space and Time” (2:07), “Go!” (3:37), “Summer Madness” (3:20), “Too Far” (2:55), “Nu Saigon” (1:00), “You’re My” (2:51), “So What” (1:43), and “Mood Indigo” (3:02). The total is 20:35. The Club Copy page prints 20:41. Nothing on the Bandcamp sleeve runs to four minutes, and “Nu Saigon,” a minute long, sits fifth, not as an outro. The page’s description is the warmest of the five: “Warm room. Nobody wants to leave. Vancouver late hours with jazz and soul in the flips.” The artist page puts him in Cumberland. The city in the sentence reads as an hour, not an address. “So What” titles the opening of Kind of Blue. “Mood Indigo” is Ellington’s. “Summer Madness” is also a 1974 Kool & the Gang instrumental. The sleeve does not say these tracks use those recordings. Two albums in six weeks wear the canon’s names and do not show the stitching.

Lakeshore West 23:55 is the title a stranger cannot check on Bandcamp. CC008, VCR026D3, dated 10 December 2025 on clubcopy.ca, two tracks, $3. The page says “Two cuts. Lakeshore West into Hold U. White-label heat from L.T. Drifta.” The catalogue times the title track at 3:55 and “Hold U” at 1:11. The Bandcamp address stored for the EP does not resolve, and the title is absent from the public music list. The EP exists as a Club Copy page and not, in this issue’s check, as a Bandcamp sleeve. That is distribution, not a riddle. The pages do not say what “23:55” refers to, so this piece will not. The timings do say the short form held, and that “white label” has been paired, five days later on the Bandcamp calendar, with a single that calls itself quiet.

Three 4 Zone was the late single until Get Me Down (CC020, 19 October 2026, 5:24) and If Ya Want (CC021, 21 October 2026, 8:21), each one track at $1.50. Three 4 Zone is the earlier of those singles. CC014, one track, 6:41, $1.50, electronic, released on Bandcamp on 18 October 2026. The page’s copy is almost bare: “One track from L.T. Drifta.” At 6:41 it is longer than Champion Sound and longer than any track on the Bandcamp edition of Classic Jazz, whose longest piece is “My One and Only Love” at 5:34. The autumn of 2025, on these timings, was compression. This single gives one idea more time. The title is three words and a numeral, and the page does not gloss them. Hearing “4” as a bar, or as the pulse of house, would be a guess. The verifiable change is the length, the date, and the catalogue number. CC014 arrives with other artists’ records — Forsberg, Riscape — occupying numbers in between. L.T. Drifta is not the whole label. The name is on the first number, the third, the sixth, the eighth, and the fourteenth.

Read as a shelf, the five refuse the single word “jungle,” because the release pages refuse it. A Cumberland catalogue holds a standards album, a twenty-minute electronic album, a two-track EP the outside sleeve does not currently carry, and two singles at $1.50, with the discrepancies left in print. CC001 is not the earliest public date. The jazz contents on the site are not the jazz album on Bandcamp. The warmest copy names a city the biography does not. On the evidence the pages support, the practice is short forms, borrowed titles, and one later track past six minutes. Bandcamp order is the year they appeared. Catalogue order is the story the number tells about a beginning. The index, mixes included, is L.T. Drifta on Club Copy.
