Love Deluxe

Sade, 1992. The record I leave on when people are still in the apartment.

1992epiclp

posted by cj archer · Sep 4, 2026

The crate column in this zine has been boys’ homework for too long. Dilla, Shadow, Jamal — all of it true, all of it a certain kind of listening with the lights on. Here’s the record I actually leave on. Sade, Love Deluxe, 1992. Not a museum piece. An apartment with people still in it.

You know the temperature even if you think you don’t. Bass that sits. A voice that will not raise itself to be believed. No drop, no proof, no “and then.” It sounds like someone who already knows you came over. I put it on when the house is mine and when it isn’t. It doesn’t care if you’re being brave about it. I have put it on in a towel. I have put it on while someone slept in the other room.

Half the Club Copy catalog is trying to get back to this without saying her name — Molly’s private mix, Rosco’s latch on the door, the kitchen after the club. Love Deluxe is the original of that feeling. Play haNo Ordinary Love” once without performing it for anyone. Then leave the album running. If you start moving the room around — a candle, a jacket off a chair, a window cracked because the night is still in your hair — that’s the listening we should have led with.
