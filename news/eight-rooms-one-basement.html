Eight Rooms, One Basement

From 2008 to 2012 the Guvernment was eighth in the world. Toronto’s other night was a basement under the Silver Dollar, and a tent in a park.

The club the world ranked eighth was not the room Toronto’s paper kept choosing.

Comfort Zone had been the basement after-hours under the Hotel Waverly, 484 Spadina, since 1996, the kind of club that runs after the other rooms have closed, in the space Buzz left when Buzz became Industry at King and Strachan. NOW, writing in 2020, names Dave Yarmus as the owner of the Silver Dollar and of the Zone. From 2008 to 2012 it ran on Saturday and the long-weekend Sunday.

The other scale was on the water. Charles Khabouth took 132 Queens Quay East on 1 January 1996. Denise Benson puts the complex at 60,000 square feet, previously Fresh, then RPM and the Warehouse, on a quay of factories and parking lots. The Guvernment opened that summer with a main room, the Acid Lounge, the Orange Room, The Drink, a roof, and Kool Haus. The licence started at 1,600 people, more than tripled, and a promoter renting the whole place could put 5,000 people under one roof.

In the 2008 Top 100 Clubs, DJ Mag had it at number 8, ten places up, the year Fabric was first. A labyrinth of eight rooms and a roof with the downtown in it. Armin van Buuren, then the world’s number 1 DJ, gets two lines. “If there’s one city that loves trance, it’s Toronto. And if there’s one club that knows how to rock, it’s the Guvernment.” And: “I don’t know any other club that pulls those amounts of people all year long.” Carl Cox, Sasha and Derrick Carter sit beside the trance. Zark Fatah, later a co-owner of Maison Mercer, told Benson the shorter version. “Guvernment put Toronto on the map globally.”

Gallery opened Halloween 2005, with Sydney Blu among the residents, the DJs kept on the bill week after week. Blu’s word for the sound is a dark, hard after-hours. Manzone & Strong came up in The Drink, held Gallery on Saturdays, and in 2010 moved to the main room as co-residents with Mark Oliver, the DJ Khabouth had hired to launch Spin Saturdays.

On 29 October 2008, NOW’s dance-club pick was Wrongbar, a room that barely qualified next to Circa and the Guvernment. The paper’s case was practical: hear it on a weeknight, or wait two months and hear it in a big room with everything that makes people hate clubs. On 4 November 2010 the paper gave Wrongbar the category again and printed 1279 Queen West, past the rail bridge at Dufferin. Circa was already “now-defunct.” Nav Sangha, who DJs as Nasty Nav, told the Globe he opened it in 2007, out of indie rooms and DJ rooms sharing a bad sound system at the Queenshead. By August 2012, profiled while opening east of the Don, he had won a 2010 hearing over capacity. “I used to joke that I’m doing all this stuff so that I can be the dirty old man at the disco one day. I don’t want to get kicked out.”

Footwork was the other basement, licensed for 255. Joel Smye and Stephan Philion opened it in May 2005 at 425 Adelaide West, down an unmarked alley, the windows boarded into dancefloor. Smye on a Friday there: “Its DJs played at places like The Guvernment and Comfort Zone, and had more of that pumping, hard-house vibe, which is also kind of a Toronto sound.” Through 2012 the door was the alley. On 23 April 2012 INK Entertainment announced VELD at Downsview Park, 4 and 5 August, all ages, with a 19-and-over VIP for 3,000. Deadmau5 headlined Saturday, Avicii Sunday, and one stage was the Guvernment Tent, with Mark Oliver on the Sunday bill. They printed the club’s name on canvas and took it to a park.

The ranking was an advertisement for a complex that could hold 5,000, and the basements were the nightlife. Once that name fits on a tent, the advertisement has won.
