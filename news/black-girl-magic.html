The Floor Already Knew

Honey Dijon’s Black Girl Magic is house as a family affair. Eve, the club, and a kick that doesn’t apologize.

posted by cj archer

Black Girl Magic is out today on Classic Music Company and Honey Dijon did not come to rescue house. She has been in the booth this whole time. Chicago to the world. “Work.” “In the Club” with Eve like a house diva the radio forgot it wanted. Pabllo Vittar and Urias on “Everybody.” Channel Tres. Mike Dunn. Luke Solomon. A family affair with a kick that still means something at 4am.

The girls who never left the floor already knew Black girl magic wasn’t a caption. It’s the DJ who kept the four-on-the-floor while everybody else was doing livestreams in their kitchen. Second album. It feels like a night she designed and then let you into. Mixmag will run the sets. Pitchfork will do the number. Cumberland gets the record. Put “Work” on and watch the apartment remember it has hips.

The gossip isn’t that house needed a headline this year. The gossip is that a Black trans woman from Chicago made the album the rooms actually play, and she didn’t sand off the joy to make it palatable. Play “In the Club” with the lights the colour they should be. If your shoulders drop before the vocal, that’s the review. The floor already knew. She just put it on wax.
