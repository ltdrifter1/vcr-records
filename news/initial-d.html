Initial D and the Sound of Speed

Eurobeat, tofu runs, and a music scene built on RPMs.

CJ Archer

A manga about a kid delivering tofu down a mountain at 4am shouldn’t have changed how a generation heard music. Initial D did. Shuichi Shigeno’s AE86 in Gunma took eurobeat with it — fast, glossy, Italo-descended dance music North America wasn’t supposed to need.

It became the default soundtrack for anyone who pointed a controller at a PlayStation, sat too close to a CRT, or taped that white Toyota above a bed. The music was doing some of the driving. RPMs as a scene, not a postcard of Japan.

If you still hear a four-on-the-floor and think of a downhill, that’s the lineage. It never asked permission to be serious.
