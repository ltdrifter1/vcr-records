CUMBERLAND

Fell Asleep on the Keys

On 14 November 2026 Archie released Get-U-Now, eighteen tracks tagged speed garage, from Cumberland. The about line on the Ninajirachi bootleg, dated 9 November, is a line from the song.

Archie’s Bandcamp gives a town and one sentence. The location line says Cumberland, British Columbia. The bio says “A warning against material pursuit at the expense of one’s inner spirit.” On 14 November 2026 the same account released Get-U-Now, catalogue CC060 on the label site, eighteen tracks at nine Canadian dollars. The tags are electronic, house, speed garage, UK garage, ukg, and Cumberland. The album page prints the date and the price. It does not print a writer, a producer, or an about line. The sleeve in the catalogue is a glossy pink car from the rear quarter, two heart-shaped taillights in pink LEDs, chrome exhausts, and the Club Copy mark in the corner. A small line on the lamp reads “LUXY AUTO.”

The running order on the album page is “give it away,” “let me love you,” “say it,” “star-six-nine,” “Left You,” “Magic Wand (3AM VIP),” “We’ve Lost,” “Want You,” “Together (3AM VIP),” “Jazz As Weapon,” “False Teeth,” “Beatback (3AM VIP),” “Get-U-Now,” “The Jamz,” “Far Away,” “Losing You,” “call ur name,” and “Interstellar Love.” Three titles carry the parenthesis “3AM VIP.” “Get-U-Now” is also a single, dated 13 November, and so are “The Jamz” and “call ur name.” The label’s page for that last one is titled “call ur name, girl.” The Bandcamp track is “call ur name.” The other fifteen titles, on this pass through the music page, live on the album.

The week before the album, three singles name somebody else’s record. On 9 November, Ninajirachi - iPod Touch (Archie’s Garage Bootleg). The same day, Jhené Aiko - So Good (Archie Bootleg). On 10 November, St Germain - Rose Rouge (Molly’s Hazy Edit: Jorja Smith Version). Those pages use the same tags as the album. Their credits fields are empty. The claim is in the title: an edit, a version, or a bootleg, and a name.

The line from the song

Ninajirachi’s “iPod Touch” came out on NLV Records on 20 June 2025, catalogue NLVR0243, performed by Ninajirachi, written by Nina Wilson and Darcy Baylis, produced by Ninajirachi and Darcy Baylis, mixed by Wave Racer, mastered by Wayne Sunderland. The single is the song at 3:16, plus an instrumental and an acapella. Wikipedia files it as the third single from her debut album I Love My Computer, and says Wilson’s real name is Nina Wilson. The lyric printed on her Bandcamp page includes the line “Fl studio so late I fell asleep on the keys.” Archie’s about line, on the bootleg dated 9 November 2026, reads “FL studio so late, fell asleep on the keys.” The duration on Archie’s page is a little under four minutes. The sentence is not a caption invented for the edit. It is a line from the record the title names, comma and capital letters aside.

The rest of that lyric is a list of what the song sounds like. A song nobody knows. A post heard at twelve. A cracked screen. FL Studio left running. Wilson told the story behind it to the Sydney Morning Herald, as Wikipedia cites her: she heard Madeon’s “Pop Culture” in the background of a YouTube tie-dye tutorial at that age. The about line Archie prints is the line in which the singer falls asleep on the instrument. A garage bootleg that puts those words in the about field is pointing at the vocal, not at a mood.

Rose Rouge, then a shorter edit

Blue Note’s own announcement, dated 10 June 2020, says Jorja Smith’s version of “Rose Rouge” came out that day, as the lead single from Blue Note Re:imagined, with the album to follow on 16 October. The same note says the track first appeared on St Germain’s 2000 Blue Note album Tourist, and that St Germain’s record was itself based on a sample of Marlena Shaw’s “Woman of the Ghetto,” from her 1973 album Cookin’ With Blue Note At Montreux. On the Tourist master, Discogs and MusicBrainz give “Rose Rouge” at 7:02. Smith’s single, on the Blue Note page, is a new recording by a Brit Award-winning singer, cued from soul, jazz, and reggae, on a compilation of UK musicians reworking the catalogue. Ezra Collective’s “Footprints” is named in the same announcement, beside Shorter, Hancock, Hutcherson, Henderson, Byrd, Tyner, and Andrew Hill.

Archie’s title, on 10 November 2026, stacks the whole chain into one line and then adds a name the Blue Note page does not use: “Molly’s Hazy Edit,” and “Jorja Smith Version.” The duration on the Archie page comes in under four minutes, against St Germain’s seven. There is no about line and no credit. The title is doing the work the credits field leaves empty. It says which record, which later version, and whose edit. It does not say who played on it, or who cleared it. A listener who wants the genealogy can still walk it, because Blue Note printed the genealogy in 2020 and the 2026 title points at the last step.

So Good, and the sentences that do not quote

Pitchfork’s Jazz Monroe, on 11 September 2026, reported Jhené Aiko’s album Westside Whimsy out, and with it “So Good,” a new verse from Kendrick Lamar. Beatport lists that recording at 3:57, 89 BPM, E-flat major, Def Jam, released the same day. Archie’s bootleg is dated 9 November 2026. The title names Jhené Aiko and the song. It does not name Lamar. The about line is “Describing friendship as a single soul inhabiting two bodies.” That sentence describes a description. It does not name Aiko, Lamar, or the song. The bootleg’s claim to the record is the title. The about line is a gloss standing next to it.

Three other November singles on the same account do the same kind of gloss, and they do not name a record in the title. We’re All Mad Here, 9 November, takes its title from the Cheshire Cat in chapter 6 of Lewis Carroll’s Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland. The Cat’s line, in the 1866 text, is “we’re all mad here. I’m mad. You’re mad.” The about line is “I knew who I was this morning, but I’ve changed a few times since then.” In chapter 5 Alice tells the Caterpillar something longer and less tidy: “I—I hardly know, sir, just at present—at least I know who I was when I got up this morning, but I think I must have been changed several times since then.” The single uses the famous compression. The book’s sentence is still there to be checked. Wake Up Mr. Mac, 6 November, says “Comparing a bookless room to a soulless body.” Outta Tha Woods, 9 November, says “On the necessity of learning how to let loved ones go rather than simply holding on.” Give Everything, the same day, says “Defining the distinct roles of the mind, heart, and soul.” That last sentence is also the entire bio on DW’s Bandcamp. DW’s one Club Copy track, Lions’ Gate, is dated 14 November 2026, the same day as Get-U-Now, and the track page has no about line of its own.

The tag

Speed garage is the tag Archie puts on the album and on the bootlegs. Todd Edwards, talking to Emma Warren at the Red Bull Music Academy in 2013, said he had thought he was making house with a shuffle. England sped the tempo up. The phrase that got attached was “speed garage,” and he did not hear it as praise. Archie’s pages use that phrase as a filing word for a set of titles. Some of the titles are ordinary speech: say it, want you, losing you, far away. Three of them are marked “3AM VIP.” Three more, off the album and dated just before it, name a 2025 Australian single, a 2020 British cover of a 2000 French house record built on a 1973 vocal, and a 2026 R&B single with a second voice the bootleg title leaves out.

The album page stops at eighteen titles, a price, a town, and the warning in the bio. The singles go further when the title says whose song it is, and once, on the Ninajirachi bootleg, when the about line repeats the song. The pink car does not add a credit. The line about falling asleep on the keys does.
