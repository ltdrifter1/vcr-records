The Camera Got a Landlord

DICE sold Boiler Room to Superstruct. The rooms started asking who owns the webcam.

posted by cj archer

DICE sold Boiler Room today to Superstruct Entertainment — the European live giant KKR bought last summer for a reported $1.4 billion. Undisclosed sum. Blaise Bellville’s webcam, the one that made a London basement feel like a passport, now reports to a festival conglomerate. DICE keeps the tickets. Superstruct keeps the brand. The rooms keep the night, if they want it.

A billion views. A hundred and twenty markets. The grainy rectangle that turned a DJ into a rumour you forwarded to the group chat. That was the trick: you could be in Peckham and also on a wet pavement in Cumberland, watching the same drop, performing the same yes. The gossip isn’t that a platform got sold. Platforms get sold. The gossip is that the underground spent fifteen years letting a camera stand in for a door, and the camera just got a landlord with a portfolio.

Some lineups will keep saying yes. Some already started saying no. Play the last good stream you actually remember — not the one you screenshotted — and ask whether you were in the room or just watching someone else be in it. The night never lived in the rectangle. The rectangle just got a new letterhead.
