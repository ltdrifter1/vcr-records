Boiler Room Got Sold

On 15 January 2025 DICE sold Boiler Room to Superstruct. The stream had a landlord. The night never had.

posted by cj archer · Jan 15, 2025

Back Catalogue · 15 Jan 2025

format
Essay
date
Jan 15, 2025
words
731 words · 3 min

On 15 January 2025, DICE sold Boiler Room to Superstruct Entertainment. Superstruct is the live-music group KKR bought, in a deal reported at about $1.4 billion, in the summer of 2024. The sale price of Boiler Room itself was not disclosed. DICE kept the ticketing relationship in the framing of the deal; Superstruct kept the brand. Blaise Bellville’s project — a camera in a room, a DJ, a comment section that made a basement feel like a passport — now reported to a festival conglomerate owned by private equity. That is a change of letterhead. It is also the logical end of a fifteen-year bet that a rectangle could stand in for a door.

The bet was not stupid. Boiler Room solved a real problem. Most people who care about dance music do not live in the city where a given set is happening, and most clubs will not pay to fly them there. A fixed camera and a decent sound feed let a teenager in another country study a DJ’s hands. At its best, the form was a lesson: track selection in real time, the boredom between peaks, the way a room leans. At its worst, it was a stage play about underground culture, with the crowd cast as extras and the DJ as a brand that could be clipped. Both versions traveled. The company’s own figures, repeated in coverage of the sale, talked about a billion views and well over a hundred markets. Reach is not a moral category. It is the reason a conglomerate wanted the name.

What the underground gave up, in exchange for the reach, was the ability to pretend the camera was neutral. A camera changes the set. DJs play records they are willing to have archived. Crowds perform having a good time, which is not the same as having one. Promoters book lineups that will travel as video. None of this is unique to one company — every phone in the room was already doing a cruder version — but Boiler Room industrialized it and gave it a house style. The house style became, for a decade, the global image of “a DJ in a small room,” even when the room was a sponsored set built for the lens. When the style was sold, people who had used it as their doorway felt the doorway change owners. The feeling is legitimate. The doorway was never theirs. It was a media company.

KKR’s ownership of Superstruct is the part that makes the story about capital rather than about a founder cashing out, though it is also about a founder cashing out. Private equity buys predictable cash flows and brands that can be slotted into a portfolio of festivals and venues. A livestream brand fits if the livestream is a funnel into live events and sponsorships. The music, in that model, is the content that keeps the brand warm. DJs who played Boiler Room for the love of a format now have to decide whether the format’s new landlord is a landlord they will work for. Some will say yes because the audience is there. Some will say no because the audience was never the same thing as the owner. Both decisions are adult. The childish one is shock that a media company could be sold.

The night, as opposed to the stream, was unaffected in the only way that matters and affected in the way that doesn’t. Unaffected: a good set in a real room on 16 January 2025 did not need the brand. Affected: the imaginary global room, the one where everyone was supposedly together because they were watching the same feed, had its deed transferred. Imaginary rooms are part of how this culture reproduces. Losing one to a balance sheet is worth writing down. It is not worth confusing with the loss of an actual club. Actual clubs close for rent, licenses, and noise complaints. Those are different landlords.

Put on a set you remember for the records, not for the angle of the shot. If you cannot separate them, the camera did its job too well. The rectangle has a new owner. The selection, if it was any good, belongs to the person who made it and to anyone who learned a transition from it. That is a smaller commons than the comment section promised. It is the one that was real.
