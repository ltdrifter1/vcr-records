No Fun City Still Raids the Rooms

Vancouver sells vibrant nightlife to the visitor brochure, then inspects the rooms that actually dance.

posted by cj archer · 12 sep 26

Vancouver will sell you a night. It will not, as a habit, let you keep the room that made one.

You already know the split. Granville gets the postcard and the late licence. Kingsway gets the inspector. Mount Pleasant gets the arts licence that turns out to be a leash. East Van gets the warehouse until somebody pulls the paper. Same city. Two nightlives. One of them is for the visitor. The other one is for you, until it isn't.

Take Your Time Back sat at 648 Kingsway and did the unglamorous thing a scene actually needs: it stayed open. Georgia Straight, 9 May 2026, put numbers on the run the chat already felt — something like four hundred shows across roughly eighteen months, starting October 2024. Unlicensed concerts. Liquor. The combination this city has been punishing since before anyone said No Fun City with a straight face. They shut preemptively in March 2026 after inspector attention. Not a last-night myth. A preemptive close. The incubator clocked out because the clipboard was coming.

Vancity Lookout, 7 January 2026, ran the enforcement ledger. Enhanced enforcement: forty-eight coordinated inspections since June 2025. Thirty-one municipal tickets. 19 December 2025, a joint ramp-up — Vancouver Fire Rescue Services, Vancouver Police, the Liquor and Cannabis Regulation Branch, the whole trinity that shows up when a city wants a room to feel watched. That is not a vibe. That is a calendar.

Six days before the joint ramp-up, Birdhouse. 13 December 2025. Queer Mount Pleasant room on an arts licence. Something like eight officials, four of them VPD, according to the coverage. Paige Frewer's line, the one that travelled: "fully giving raid." Two fire tickets, disputed. You can argue the tickets. You cannot undo the night the uniforms walked in and the floor learned, again, what this city thinks a queer arts room is for.

Vantek, the East Van warehouse at 236 Industrial, went earlier that same stretch. Shut in early December 2025 after fines, arts-and-culture licence pulled. Warehouse, industrial address, the kind of building a city forgets until a bassline reminds it. Then the licence becomes a story the city tells about why the music had to stop.

File the politics with the hedge they deserve. The Straight, summarizing Lookout, had Councillor Brian Montague's 2025 email allegedly listing five "illegal" venues from industry tips. Allegedly. Industry tips. You know what that phrase means if you have ever sat in a booking meeting that turned into a morality play. Twelve venues targeted June through December 2025, same summary. Night Shift is not going to pretend it sat in the inbox. The reporting is the reporting. The shape is the shape: names moving from a room to a desk to a coordinated visit.

A city can print vibrant and still send fire, police, and liquor to the rooms that were never going to make the asset report.

You have been in the room they mean. Not the branded one. The one with the folding table and the someone-knows-someone door. House that did not ask permission to be house. Jungle that did not wait for a patio application. Instrumental hip-hop in a warehouse that smelled like dust and a borrowed sub. Cascadia has always built nights like that. The Pacific Northwest does not have a surplus of legal dance floors. It has a surplus of weather and a talent for making a night in spite of the bylaw.

No Fun City is not a cute nickname when the inspections hit forty-eight. It is a municipal personality. The personality has a tell: it will tolerate the night it can invoice, and it will treat the night it cannot invoice as a safety problem.

Safety is the word they will use. Keep it on the desk. The city frames enforcement as safety and major-event readiness — the Vancouver Sun had that language; a Swiss New Year's Eve fire lives in the citation — and nobody with a brain wants a room that burns. That is not the argument. The argument is which rooms get the coordinated visit and which rooms get the 4 a.m. extension. Granville can go late when the visitor numbers are up. Kingsway gets preemptively closed after a year and a half of showing the city what an incubator looks like.

The FIFA year made the split louder, not smarter. The strip posted sales. The warehouses posted shutdowns. Do not invent a conspiracy. Do not pretend the readiness fell evenly.

If you live here and you care about the floor — not the patio, the floor — you already did the math. Four hundred shows. Forty-eight inspections. Thirty-one tickets. A queer arts room "fully giving raid." A warehouse licence pulled. An alleged list of five, a reported twelve. March 2026, TYTB gone before the inspector had to finish the thought.

The city still wants to be seen dancing. It just prefers you do it where the light is good and the paper is in order. Vibrant in the copy. Raid in the room.
