Inferno

The Boards of Canada discography lists a fifth studio album, Inferno, released 29 May 2026 on Warp. The UK albums peak in that table is 3. This page does not describe the music.

A discography is a public object, and it can be ahead of a reviewer’s ears. The Wikipedia discography of Boards of Canada, a page that also lists the 1998 debut and the 2002 and 2013 albums this magazine has dated from the same table, includes a fifth studio album: Inferno, released 29 May 2026, on Warp, as CD, vinyl, and digital download. The UK albums-chart peak printed in that row is 3. Australia is 3. The United States figure in the row is 29. BPI certifications are not printed in the cell the way gold and silver are printed for the earlier albums. This essay will not supply a certification the cell leaves blank, and it will not supply a description of the record’s sound. The desk has the table. The table is the piece.

The same page lists “Deep Time” in 2026 as a promotional video attached to Inferno. A second 2026 line, “Introit” / “Prophecy at 1420 MHz,” is tagged as a digital download in the singles table. This page will not assign that second title to the album unless a later correction of the table makes the album cell explicit. One video credited to the album is enough to show that the campaign had a public image as well as a release date. Scottish, the word the discography uses for the duo at the top of the page, still applies. Warp, the label of Geogaddi and Tomorrow’s Harvest and the co-label of the debut, is the label again. The independent Skam credit of 1998 is not repeated in the 2026 row.

Number 3 in the UK, if the table holds, is a different kind of arrival from number 193 in 1998 or number 21 in 2002 or number 7 for Tomorrow’s Harvest in 2013. The catalogue’s chart positions have risen as the catalogue has aged. That sentence is only as good as the table. Tables on a public encyclopedia are corrected when they are wrong. A magazine that prints them should say where it read them and what it refused to add. What this magazine refuses to add is a listening account of a record it is treating as a dated catalogue entry in the month the entry gives. May 2026 already contains, in this volume, a 2001 rap album and a 1997 drum-and-bass single. Inferno does not have to converse with them. It has to be findable in the month its release date claims.

If the date or the peak is revised at the source, this page should be revised with it. That is a dull promise and the right one. Night Shift is not using a new album as atmosphere. It is using a discography row as a fact, with the row’s limits left in the prose. Warp. 29 May 2026. CD, vinyl, digital. UK peak 3, as listed. A video called “Deep Time.” No invented tracklist, no borrowed adjectives from the 1998 album, no claim that a hills studio was used again. The Pentland Hills belong to the debut’s page. They are not automatically the fifth album’s room.

Sources

  • Wikipedia discography, studio-album table: Inferno, 29 May 2026, Warp, UK peak 3; “Deep Time” listed as a 2026 promotional video for the album.
