Volume II

Aphex Twin’s Selected Ambient Works Volume II came out on Warp on 7 March 1994. Most of the pieces were untitled. The CD omitted a track the vinyl kept.

Selected Ambient Works Volume II was released on 7 March 1994 through Warp, on double CD, double cassette, and triple LP in the United Kingdom. A Sire double CD followed on 12 April in Australia, Japan, and the United States. The UK CD length is given as 156 minutes and 42 seconds. The LP and cassette run 166 minutes and 53 seconds. The difference is not a remaster. Wikipedia says the 19th track was left off the original CD pressings because it did not fit. Format, here, changed the contents. Anyone who knows the album from the CD knows a shorter work than the person who bought the vinyl.

The first Selected Ambient Works, 85–92, had come out on 9 November 1992 on Apollo, an R&S imprint. Volume II keeps the title and changes the brief. The page says it is largely ambient, where the debut was ambient techno, and that Richard D. James likened it to standing in a power station on acid. That comparison is his, reported on the page, and this piece will not extend it. Most tracks were untitled and marked with photographs and pie charts. Fan-made titles circulated afterward. The official condition of the album is untitled pieces. Using the fan titles as if they were printed is a way of losing the design. The design was the refusal of titles.

It entered the CIN Dance Albums Chart at number one and stayed in the top five for six weeks. It entered the albums chart at number 11. By July 1994 it had sold more than 60,000 copies outside the United States. Those are the commercial facts attached to a double album of mostly untitled ambient pieces, issued in March, in three formats that did not all contain the same number of tracks. The dance-chart number one is the fact that stops the record being filed only as “music for another room.” A dance chart is a shop category. Warp put the album where that category could count it, and the category put it at number one. Ambient, in March 1994, was not outside the dance market. It was, for six weeks, at the top of one of that market’s lists.

The missing CD track is the detail a collector’s magazine should not hurry past. A canon built on the CD is a canon with a hole the vinyl does not have. Later expanded editions, listed at 184 minutes and 53 seconds, try to close the hole. The 1994 object is the one with the hole still open, and with the hole depending on which object you bought. Metadata is not a courtesy on this album. It is the only tracklist the official release was willing to offer: pictures, charts, a format, and a length that changes when the format changes.
