Geogaddi

Geogaddi is the second studio album on the Boards of Canada discography, released 13 February 2002, on Warp, as CD, vinyl, and a later digital download. The UK peak in that table is 21. Ireland is 28. France is 78. The certification column says BPI silver. The first album, Music Has the Right to Children, had peaked at 193 in the UK and is listed as BPI gold, on Warp, Skam, and Matador, dated 20 April 1998. Four years sit between a record that barely entered the albums chart and a record that reached 21. The gap is the story the numbers can tell without a description of the synthesizers.

The duo are Scottish, in the wording at the top of the same page. This essay will not move Hexagon Sun from the debut album’s entry onto this one. The Geogaddi line does not name a studio. It names a label, a date, formats, and peaks. Warp, by 2002, was the label. Skam, which had issued the January 1998 Aquarius 7-inch and Hi Scores, is not in the Geogaddi label cell. The path from a 500-copy single to a silver album is visible in the table if you read down the page instead of starting at the most famous row.

Silver is a sales certification, not a critical rank. Gold on the debut and silver on the follow-up do not mean the first record was “better” and sold less, or the reverse. They mean the industry’s thresholds and the life of the catalogue did not move in the same direction as the chart peaks. A record can peak at 193 and later be certified gold because people kept buying it. A record can peak at 21 and be certified silver because the threshold and the moment differed. The table is allowed to be slightly counterintuitive. That is what tables are for.

February 2002 is also far enough from February 1995 that the two do not explain each other. Maxinquaye and Geogaddi share a month on a calendar and almost nothing else: different countries’ industries, different labels, different decades. Putting them in one volume is a consequence of the month, not a claim that downtempo in London and the Warp catalogue in 2002 are one practice. They are two objects a reader can hold in the same February. The discography’s refusal to list a studio for the second album is a small mercy. It stops a writer from inventing a room.
