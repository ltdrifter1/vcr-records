Eclipse

There’s an old Japanese story that says the sun and moon once loved each other, but time and distance kept them apart. So the universe made the eclipse — a brief window where they could meet.

That might be why eclipses feel the way they do. Not because they’re rare as spectacle, but because they’re rare as timing: two distant things lined up for a little while, then gone again.

Some people come into a life like daylight — obvious, warming, hard to miss. Others stay quieter, like the moon: farther off, harder to hold, impossible to forget. You don’t always get to keep what you love. Sometimes you get one look, one night, one stretch of music that proves the feeling was real even if the timing wasn’t kind.

I’m writing this after watching the sky go strange for a few minutes and then return to ordinary. It left me thinking about the Club Copy records we put out for those same kinds of hours — short meetings that stay longer than they should. Press play if you need one.
